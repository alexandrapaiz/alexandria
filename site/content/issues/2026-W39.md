# Harness distillation now works without the harness at runtime

*The latest in AI research, read in full and distilled weekly: what's new, what's gaining acceptance, and what newer evidence has overturned.*


The agent-building world has spent the week arguing about scaffolding: the extra instructions and tooling that make frontier models reliable on real tasks. The uncomfortable truth is that the best harnesses are too complex to ship, and the simple ones leave too much performance on the table. This week, one group showed how to distill the complex harness into the model itself, so the deployment harness can stay minimal. Another traced what happens when you train agents in stages, and the picture is not pretty. And a regularized search method is making harness evolution less of a lottery.

Today: a distillation method that moves a model from 23.3% to 44.3% task success while stripping away the evolved harness that made it possible. A multi-stage training study where later stages wipe out earlier ones. And a recursive harness-improvement loop that prunes itself as it searches.

## The harness can be taught, then removed

Researchers at Peking University, Google, and HKUST have found a way to make a model absorb the behavioral patterns of an elaborate harness without needing that harness after training. Their method, Harness-Zero, uses a second agent during data collection only: this "harnessing agent" reviews each of the student model's proposed actions against a private reference harness, passing good ones through and rewriting bad ones with the smallest valid replacement. The student never sees the complex harness directly. It only sees the corrected trajectories.

The result is stark. The base model scores 23.3% macro-average task success. With the evolved harness attached at deployment, it reaches 41.7%. The distilled model, running under only the minimal target harness, hits **44.3%** — higher than the model that still carries the full scaffolding. The authors measured this across spreadsheet work, multi-application tool use, and scientific reasoning, and the distilled model recovers **82.3%** of the harness-induced behavioral patterns on average.

The supervision signal matters enormously. Harness-guided review during trajectory collection yields 30% macro-average success after distillation. Alternative supervision sources — direct stronger-model trajectories, trajectories generated under the optimized harness, review without the private reference, or review based only on final answers — achieve only 3% to 15%. The mechanism is the message.

**The number that matters:** 44.3% with the harness gone, up from 23.3%.

Teaching the structure beats leaving it attached, which is the part nobody expected.

*Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness* - [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24974)

## What happens when you train agents in stages

A team led by Haixin Wang and colleagues tested what multi-stage post-training does to agent capabilities. They trained on search tasks, then math, then e-commerce, then instruction-following, measuring after each stage. The findings are a warning for anyone building compound systems.

After the search stage, single-hop search scores 45.2. After e-commerce training, it collapses to 14.6 — a **30.6 point drop**. Multi-hop search falls from 37.4 to 9.4, a **28.0 point drop**. The final instruction-following stage partially recovers earlier capabilities: single-hop search climbs back to 33.5, still 11.7 points below its peak. Multi-hop search recovers to 25.0, remaining 12.4 points short.

The authors traced the mechanism. Different post-training tasks push the model's weights in distinct directions in parameter space. Each stage's checkpoint moves toward its single-task oracle, but no checkpoint can align perfectly with multiple oracles. The geometry prevents it.

For builders: sequential specialization is not free. If your agent needs search and math and tool use, training them in sequence will destroy what came before. The recovery is partial at best.

*ACLArena: Agent Continue Learning in Multi-stage Post-training* - [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23989)

## A search method that prunes its own complexity

Researchers at Google Cloud AI Research, Stanford, and Washington University in St. Louis have regularized the recursive improvement of agent harnesses. Their system, RRSI, adds three constraints to harness evolution: structural pruning that deletes components with no measured positive gain, a stall detector that diversifies search when progress flatlines, and a selector that rejects candidates for benchmark leakage, noise-adjusted score floors, or poor cost-to-gain ratio.

The gains are measured. RRSI improves in-distribution performance by up to **14.1 points** and out-of-distribution by up to **4.7 points** across five held-out benchmarks, while using **30% fewer policy tokens** than unregularized evolution. The annealed edit budget starts broad and narrows following a cosine schedule, so early exploration gives way to late refinement.

The procedure is extractable: initialize, evaluate, generate candidates with bounded edits, filter by three non-compensatory criteria, select, prune, repeat. The authors make the steps explicit enough to implement.

*RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses* - [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972)

## What the field is leaving behind

The belief that small open-source VLMs need substantial fine-tuning for robot deployment took a hit. A team showed that a well-designed semantic interface alone — discrete action units that a foundation VLM can reason over directly — unlocks substantial embodied capability without added model capacity or embodiment-specific pretraining. The earlier claim that "only a few GPU-hours" were needed now looks like unnecessary work.

*Show-Harness: Just a VLM Agent Can Play Robots* - [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10522)

Several earlier approaches to harness and model co-evolution are being superseded by more specific mechanisms. The general claim that weaker-model harness evolution improves enterprise tasks now breaks into finer-grained operations: on-policy correction for failed turns, sequential intervention to identify which agent change resolves a failure, and harness distillation that removes the evolved scaffold at deployment. The field is moving from "co-evolve" as a slogan to named operations with measurable effects.
