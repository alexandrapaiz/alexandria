# alexandria digest — 2026‑W37  

The week’s research sharpens two converging themes. First, **feedback‑rich environments** are emerging as a practical way to tame long‑horizon training, letting agents internalize guidance instead of relying on brittle inference‑time tricks. The claim that FEEs boost self‑evolving agents on both SciWorld and BFCL (see below) exemplifies this shift. Second, **modular, production‑grade system designs** are gaining traction. Papers on Miles, NeoHorse, and MaxKernel all showcase architectures that separate concerns—routing, sub‑agents, or rollout engines—so that large‑scale RL loops can be built, debugged, and scaled more reliably. Both currents point toward a future where we treat the training pipeline itself as a first‑class engineering artifact.

## Trailblazing  

**Feedback‑Enriched Environments (FEEs) consistently raise performance on long‑horizon tasks.**  
Why it matters: Adding structured feedback to observations lets self‑evolving agents learn more efficiently, reducing the need for hand‑crafted reward shaping.  
*Environments as Scaffold: Enriching Feedback to Bootstrap Self‑Evolving Agents in Long‑Horizon Tasks* — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08404)

**Intra‑group feedback consistency is a stability boundary for agents in FEEs.**  
Why it matters: Keeping feedback signals consistent within a training group prevents divergence, making large‑scale runs more predictable.  
*Environments as Scaffold: Enriching Feedback to Bootstrap Self‑Evolving Agents in Long‑Horizon Tasks* — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08404)

**Miles’ rollout engines built on SGLang deliver efficient large‑scale RL execution.**  
Why it matters: Leveraging SGLang reduces overhead in asynchronous rollout pipelines, freeing compute for higher‑level policy updates.  
*Miles v0.1: Production‑Level Post‑Training* — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08368)

**NeoHorse’s routing harness lifts macro‑average benchmark scores by 5–6 points for 4B/9B models.**  
Why it matters: Intelligent routing of model capabilities into a training mixture yields measurable gains without extra model parameters.  
*NeoHorse‑1: Towards Recursive Self‑Improvement via Agentic Post‑Training with Routing Harness* — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08183)

**MaxKernel’s multi‑agent system matches expert‑hand‑tuned TPU kernels on JaxBench.**  
Why it matters: Automated kernel generation removes a major engineering bottleneck while preserving performance, opening the door to broader hardware‑aware agentic pipelines.  
*MaxKernel: Agentic Kernel Generation for TPUs* — [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04523)

## Gaining traction  

*Puffin‑World* unifies physics, geometry, and appearance as native world states, eliminating external modules. The three independent citations indicate growing confidence that a single unified representation can replace fragmented pipelines.  

*Compile‑by‑training* turns natural‑language specs into reusable neural functions that run without teacher models at inference. Three supporting claims suggest the community sees this as a viable path to model‑driven code generation.  

*Inference‑time context management* (limited tool set, fixed judge) shows larger gains than most system tweaks across benchmarks, backed by three citations—signaling that smarter context handling is a high‑impact lever.  

*One‑shot on‑policy distillation (OPD)* improves for hundreds of steps from a single query, recovering most of full‑data gains; two supporting claims highlight its efficiency for rapid adaptation.  

*Random Attention* evicts KV cache entries uniformly at random, matching the strongest prior evictor while boosting throughput by 32‑143% on vLLM deployments; two citations confirm its practical relevance.
