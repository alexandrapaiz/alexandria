failure is therefore somewhere in that unguarded middle, and the Modal

## 4. "Gaining traction" counts a paper citing itself

```sql
with sup as (
  select l.to_claim, count(*) n, count(distinct cf.paper_id) d
  from claim_links l
    join claims cf on cf.id = l.from_claim
    join claims ct on ct.id = l.to_claim
  where l.relation = 'supports' group by 1)
select count(*) filter (where n >= 2)            as traction_candidates,
       count(*) filter (where n >= 2 and d = 1)  as own_paper_only,
       count(*) filter (where n >= 2 and d >= 2) as independently_backed
from sup;
-- 25 | 12 | 13
```

Twelve of the twenty-five claims that qualify for "Gaining traction"
are supported only by the paper that made them. Worked through to the
published issue:

| digest line | claim | supports | distinct papers |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Compile-by-training turns natural-language specs into reusable neural functions" | 73 | 3 | **1 (itself)** |
| "Using 16 diverse queries in OPD reaches 98.9% state coverage" | 45 | 2 | **1 (itself)** |
| "One-shot OPD on a single query recovers most performance gains" | 43 | 2 | **1 (itself)** |
| "Iris shows that inference-time context management outperforms..." | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| "RISE's loop that pairs outcome-based RLVR rewards..." | 31 | 4 | 3 |

The issue printed "(3 supports)" against the first and "(3 supports)"
against Iris. One of those is four independent papers agreeing; the
other is one paper repeating itself. The reader is given the same
two-word label for both.

`sandbox|isolat|containment|escape|privilege|permission|jailbreak|exfiltrat|untrusted|injection`
returns 9 rows, and all 9 are other senses of the word: RL *privileged

The nearest real cluster is claims 386-390, **Emergence World:
Adversarial Stress-Testing of Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Systems**
(arxiv:2609.17320, tier b, 2026-09-15), all five carrying procedures.
It has **zero edges** in the claim graph.

The three papers `sources.yaml` named on 2026-09-19 as the ones cs.CR
was added to reach — 2609.15906, 2609.17648, 2609.14079 — are all
absent from the corpus. `ARXIV_MAX_PER_CAT = 100` with
`sortBy=submittedDate descending` gives cs.CR (~34 papers/day, per the
file's own comment) under three days of reach, and no arXiv rows were
fetched on 2026-09-19 or 2026-09-20. The category itself is now working:
2026-09-21 brought in 11 security papers including "CIPL: A
Channel-Aware Framework for Recoverable Privacy Leakage in LLM Agents"
and "Micro-Collaborative Poisoning: A Distributed Attack on RAG
Systems". The diff was right; three named papers fell through the window
while it waited to reach production.
