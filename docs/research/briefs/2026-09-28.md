## 5. Claim-graph error hunt

**Six `contradicts` edges exist in the whole graph. I judge four wrong.**

| edge | verdict |
|---|---|
| 12 -> 11 | **Wrong.** Same paper. Expert ceiling beside measured score, one results table. |
| 190 -> 188 | **Wrong.** Same paper. A paper recorded as contradicting itself. |
| 85 -> 12 | **Wrong.** Different papers, different systems, different benchmarks, and "the same benchmark" resolved across a paper boundary. |
| 265 -> 85 | **Wrong.** Two different systems on RMBench. That is a comparison. |
| 82 -> 5 | Doubtful. Embodied VLA against search. Different task shapes; a comparison at best. |
| 136 -> 129 | Defensible. Different benchmarks, but a genuine disagreement about whether enriched environment feedback helps. |

Two of six are intra-paper. That matters structurally, because
`deprecated_claims` is defined as any claim targeted by a `contradicts` edge
at confidence >= 0.7, with **no check that the contradicting claim is newer
and no check that it comes from a different paper**, though the view's own
comment says "a newer claim contradicts it". All six targets are therefore
marked deprecated, including claim 11 - *tau^tau-Bench*'s central result,
deprecated by its own paper's other half.

`refines` is the larger and quieter problem. 112 edges, 27 cross-paper, and
the sample in section 4 shows the failure mode: a number of similar magnitude
is treated as a refinement of a different number. These edges are published
as "the approaches being superseded", so a loose `refines` is a public claim
that something was replaced.

### The fix exists, is correct, and has never run

`prompts/interpret.md` was revised on 2026-09-19 (commit a94a003, "Meta-review:
sharpen interpret.md on contradictions, anaphora and refines"). It adds:
a rule that co-reported results are never conflicts; a rule that "the same X"
resolves inside its own paper and never across papers; and a tightening of
`refines` that says in its own words that a loose one "announces a
replacement that never happened".

It carries, as its worked example, **claim 85 against claim 12** - the exact
pair still live in the graph - and gives the correct answer as `unrelated`.

HEAD hashes to `6706ec7bffee`. All 257 edges in `claim_links`, created
2026-09-08 through **2026-09-28**, carry `openai/gpt-oss-120b@fbe080261d6b`,
which is the file as it stood on 2026-09-07. The revision has produced zero
edges in nine days.

It is not only that old errors persist. Edge 265 `contradicts` 85 was created
on **2026-09-26**, seven days after the fix merged, and it is precisely the

**Per the charter, I am not proposing into `interpret.md`.** Stacking a

## 6. Three more structural findings

**6.1 The topics vocabulary has been corrupted by non-ASCII characters.**
`prompts/distill.md` defines a closed 13-tag vocabulary. The claims table
holds 42 distinct tags over the last 14 days, including four pairs that
differ only by a U+2011 non-breaking hyphen:

| ASCII | count | non-breaking | count |
|---|---|---|---|
| `post-training` | 172 | `post-training` (U+2011) | 12 |
| `harness-engineering` | 161 | (U+2011) | 4 |
| `loop-engineering` | 90 | (U+2011) | 2 |
| `context-engineering` | 64 | (U+2011) | 1 |

A claim tagged with the non-breaking variant is invisible to any filter
written with a keyboard, and `topics` feeds the digest payload. This is
ban-list entry 13 reaching past the prose and into the database. Around
twenty-five further tags are simply off-vocabulary (`reasoning`,
`distillation`, `reward-design`, `training`, `evaluation`, `tools`). Note
that `reasoning`, the owner's named priority, exists as exactly one claim
under a tag that is not in the vocabulary.

**6.2 One of the three discovery signals cannot fire.**
`discovery_report`'s `citation_velocity_outliers` filters to
`p.tier not in ('b','c')`. `citation_log` covers 240 papers and **every one
is tier `b`**, so the query returns zero rows and always will. Two causes.
The filter's premise is inverted relative to sources.yaml's own legend, where
`b` and `c` are the strong-prior tiers and the neutral firehose is `a` and
`a-low`; the intent in source-discovery.md section 3.3 maps to `a`/`a-low`. And even
corrected it stays empty, because `check_citations` requires an `arxiv:` id
plus a triage row, tiers `c` and `d` are entirely non-arXiv feeds, and tier
`a`/`a-low` have almost no triage rows (section 1). The signal is downstream
of the same throughput problem as everything else.

**6.3 The relevance law is not implementable by the current ingest.**
`fetch_arxiv` sorts by `submittedDate` descending and takes
`ARXIV_MAX_PER_CAT = 100`. There is no fetch-by-id and no backfill path. The
corpus can therefore only ever contain what was new at the moment we looked,
and a paper that earns traction months after publication is structurally
unreachable. The charter's standing rule says a 2023 paper compounding this
month outranks yesterday's upload. We cannot currently ingest that paper.
The market seat handed us a live instance this week (section 8).
