## 2. Synthesis: the verifier was the component, every time

Three briefs ago this seat said containment had turned from a design
literature into a measurement literature. The skills literature has just
completed the same turn, and reading the 2026 line end to end -- which this run
could do only because five of its papers were in the reading queue -- the
movement is narrower and more useful than "skills are evolving."

**The field spent 2026 taking the human out of the skill loop one component at
a time, and in every ablation that was run, the component that carried the
gain was the check and not the representation.** EvoSkill (March) discovers
skills from failure analysis and gates them on a Pareto frontier over held-out
validation: +7.3 on OfficeQA, +12.1 on SealQA, +5.3 transferring unmodified to
BrowseComp. CoEvoSkills (April, and a headline of 2026-W42) replaces that gate
with a surrogate verifier that writes test assertions, reaches 71.1% on
SkillsBench against 53.5% for human curation -- and when the verifier is
removed, **drops 30 points to 41.1%, below context-only evolution at 42.4%**.
SkillDAG's ablation separates its two contributions and the typed graph is
worth 1.4 points while the online verified editing is worth 3.6. RSIGym, the
newest of them, reports that harness revision beat data-only and training-only
research "by wide margins," and the harness revisions it describes are
diagnosis-then-patch loops with verification commands added.

Four groups, four ablations, one direction. The representation is worth a
point or two; the loop that checks is worth tens.

**The retrieval camp arrived at the same answer from the other side, and this
is the part a builder should act on.** SkillRet says the retriever leaderboard
does not transfer to skill retrieval at all -- rho=0.70 against MTEB with
ranking inversions severe enough that a 270M model at 64.6 beats a 7B at 57.0.
So tuning the retriever is not the lever. What is the lever is the selection
interface: "Lost in Implicit Routing" holds tasks and golden skills fixed,
adds 1,000 irrelevant skills, and watches recall fall **73.6% to 27.4%** while
the rate of loading no skill at all rises **9.3% to 51.6%** -- with explicit
preselection from the same library holding **73.9%**. And it names the
mechanism, which neither the market seat's note nor 2026-W42 carried: masking
only the *descriptions* of the irrelevant skills largely restores loading, so
the damage is semantic interference from metadata, plus instruction
interference when choosing a skill competes with doing the task. Progressive
Disclosure is the same finding as a fix -- metadata only, explicit `LOAD_SKILL`
-- and it holds under eight hard negatives where full injection collapses.

**So the two camps are one camp.** Self-evolution says the gain is in the
check; implicit routing says the loss is in asking the model to choose while
it works. Both are statements that **an agent's judgment is the expensive,
unreliable part, and the win comes from moving a decision out of it** -- into a
verifier, or into an explicit preselection step. That is the week's thesis and
it is sharper than the digest's split, because the split is an architecture
question and this is a design rule.

**What a builder does differently this week, concretely.** If you are
spending your next increment on a better embedding model for your skill
library, stop: SkillRet says the leaderboard will not transfer and Lost in
Implicit Routing says the retriever was never the binding constraint. Spend
it on explicit preselection in front of the agent, and on a verifier behind
whatever writes your skills. If you ship more than a few dozen skills and let
the model pick implicitly from descriptions, you are at the measured part of
that curve, and the failure is silent -- your agent loads *nothing* half the
time rather than loading the wrong thing.

**And it applies to us.** Alexandria's own trigger test is a lexical stand-in
for retrieval, which is the half the evidence says is not the problem. The
mechanism is description interference at library scale, and a seven-skill
library cannot see it. Section 3 carries that to the skill seat.

**The same finding at the other scale is section 1.** A pipeline whose
representation improved for a month while its check stayed broken printed
three false supersessions to readers. The literature spent 2026 proving the
check is the component. We are the case study.

## 5. What the engineer should know, and item 1 is urgent

**1. The outage is destroying corpus reach permanently, at about 130 papers a
day, and this has not been said anywhere.** `ARXIV_MAX_PER_CAT = 100`
(`pipeline/ingest.py:36`) with `sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending` and
no date cursor means each run sees the 100 most recent per category and
nothing else. Measured against the arXiv API this run: `cs.AI` submits **116 a
day** and `cs.LG` **114**. So the overflow is lost on an ordinary day, and
across the 3.33 days since ingest's last write (2026-10-23T11:02Z, from
`/api/delivery`) **roughly 290 to 570 papers are already beyond any future
run's reach**, the range being cross-listing between the two. Six of eight
categories are inside their window and lose nothing. Two fixes, in
`docs/research/notes/2026-10-26-source-census.md` section 4: a per-category window,
and paging to the previous run's newest id, which is the one that makes any
future outage recoverable. **Escalation: this is a better argument for the
workspace than "the cron is down," because it has a rate attached.**

**2. The repair route for bad edges exists and the backlog has just moved out
of its reach.** Full chain in the audit note, section 4. In short: `latest_links` and
the 2026-10-24 index change mean a later judgment can withdraw a
contradiction, and `REJUDGE_QUEUE_SQL` triggers on `prompt_sha` alone. The
same-paper exclusion that shipped on 2026-10-25 (`5af27966`) is *shortlist
SQL*, so it moves no prompt sha and makes no existing judgment due -- and both
the fresh and rejudge paths now pass the judged claim's own `paper_id`, so a
rejudge will **never show the judge the same-paper candidate** and can never
write the corrected relation. Every same-paper `contradicts` edge written
before 2026-10-25 is therefore permanent. The backlog needs a one-off
withdrawal pass or a `retracted_at` column; it cannot be a rejudge and it
cannot be a prompt.
