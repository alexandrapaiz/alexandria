## 1. The finding that changes what we sell

**Of a skill's measured effect, the share that comes from supplying facts is
4.5 percent. The share that comes from anchoring a procedure is 65.7
percent.** *Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work -- Until They Don't*
(`arxiv:2608.14036`, 2026-08-14; Princeton, Stanford, USC, Johns Hopkins, UC
San Diego) attributes skill effects to three mechanisms over Terminal-Bench
2.0, Terminal-Bench-Pro and SkillsBench on GPT-5.3-Codex, GPT-5.4 and
Gemini-3.1-Pro: Procedural Anchoring 65.7 percent of cases, Knowledge
Injection 4.5 percent, Failure Warning the remainder. Section 5.1's sentence
is "skills primarily work as procedural anchors".

Alexandria sells nine skills whose content is research findings. On this
paper's attribution, a findings-only skill is betting on the 4.5 percent
channel. The paper reports no per-category delta, so this is a caveat over a
shelf rather than a measurement of our shelf -- but it is the first number in
reach that bounds the channel our product thesis runs through, and it is a
bound in the unflattering direction.

The honest read of our own estate is that we do not ship findings-only
skills: `harness-engineering` is three deltas each ending in an action, and
the ADR-38 rewrite cut every section the bare subject gave unprompted. That
rewrite now has external support it did not have when it was made. It is also
the nearest thing the library has to a defence, and it is worth saying out
loud that it is a defence by construction and not by measurement.

Ranked first here on evidence rather than date: three benchmarks, three
frontier models, a mechanism attribution with a percentage, and it lands
directly under the product's differentiator. Posted 2026-08-14, which is the
third-oldest item in this brief.

## 2. The library-size question has an answer, and our size is the safe part

The 2026-09-26 queue line said nothing measures skill libraries at the size a
paying library ships, between five and fifty, and that alexandria sits in an
untested middle. **That is no longer true.** *When Single-Agent with Skills
Replace Multi-Agent Systems and When They Fail* (`arxiv:2601.04748v2`,
2026-01-15; Xiaoxiao Li, TEA Lab, University of British Columbia / Vector
Institute / CIFAR AI Chair) sweeps selection accuracy over
|S| in {5, 10, 20, 35, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200} in section 5.2, figure 2, on
GPT-4o-mini and GPT-4o: above 90 percent at |S| <= 20, degrading steadily
beyond |S| = 30, about 20 percent at |S| = 200. The fitted capacity parameter
kappa is 91.8 for GPT-4o-mini and 83.5 for GPT-4o, and kappa is the half-maximum
point, not the onset -- the two sentences are consistent and a reader who
quotes kappa as the knee will be wrong by a factor of three.

Two caveats that keep this from being better than it is. The library is
**synthetic**: 200 templates over eight domains, three seeds, no benchmark
dataset. And the backbones are the 4o generation, so the vintage objection
that the queue raised about a different paper applies to this one.

Alexandria ships nine skills. On this curve, |S| = 9 sits inside the plateau,
and the degradation onset is 30 rather than 50. So the thing to worry about at
our size is not the library, and the next section says what it is instead.

## 3. The 1.1 percent headline does not survive its own benchmark

`skills/skill-library-engineering` delta 3 carries this sentence: on 177
executable tasks over 79 skills, Qwen3-32B under progressive disclosure loaded
the correct skill on 1.1 percent of them against 90.9 percent with a native
router (*The Router Within*, `arxiv:2609.15982`). The attribution is correct
and the skill quotes it correctly.

**The benchmark's own authors measure the same thing on the same 79 skills and
177 tasks and get between 32 and 97 percent.** *Skill-Use: Can LLMs Actually
Use Skills in Agentic Harnesses?* (`arxiv:2608.04828`, 2026-08-05; Han, Xu,
Liao, Wang, Jiang, Di, Lu, Hu, Xiao) table 1 reports trigger rate for eight
models under two harnesses, Claude Code and Codex:

| model | Claude Code | Codex |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-5.5 | 0.972 | 0.966 |
| Claude Opus 4.7 | 0.966 | 0.836 |
| Claude Opus 4.8 | 0.940 | 0.881 |
| MiniMax-M3 | 0.833 | 0.864 |
| Qwen3.6-Max | 0.446 | 0.684 |
| Kimi-K2.6 | 0.337 | 0.706 |
| DeepSeek-V4-Pro | 0.324 | 0.650 |
| GLM-5.1 | 0.324 | 0.706 |

Read together, three things follow and each is worth more than the headline.

**The vintage objection fails.** The 2026-10-26 queue line asked whether the
1.1 percent is a model-vintage artifact of Qwen3-32B. It is not: DeepSeek-V4-Pro
and GLM-5.1 are contemporary and sit at 0.324 under Claude Code. Low triggering
is a live property of current frontier models.

**The headline is still wrong, by a different route.** Nothing in the
benchmark's own table is within an order of magnitude of 0.011. The 1.1
percent is *The Router Within*'s reimplemented progressive-disclosure baseline
on one mid-size open model, not progressive disclosure as the benchmark's
authors ran it in two deployed harnesses. Delta 3 should keep its advice and
lose its headline -- which is exactly the disposition the queue line predicted,
for the wrong reason.

**And the queue line misattributed its own premise.** It says *The Router
Within* notes that Qwen3-32B predates the skill ecosystem the benchmark
measures. The paper says no such thing; it carries no vintage caveat on that
result at all. Fourth instance this fortnight of a quantity or a caveat
asserted about a paper by a seat that did not open it, recorded in the
incident register.

**One number that does survive, and is the one to build on.** Appendix F.1:
putting the shortlisted full bodies in the menu helps only on SRA-Bench
(+7 points) and hurts SkillRet (-2.2), at 26 to 124 seconds of prefill. The
obvious repair for a routing failure is to show the router more, and the
measurement says it does not work. That part of delta 3 is sound.

## 4. Measured here: our own routing error is one skill, not a library

Our trigger suite is a metadata-menu, all-candidates-visible, forced-choice
instrument: it scores a prompt against every skill description plus an
eight-decoy panel and takes the argmax. That is the *native-router* regime
Skill-Use scores at 0.909, not the progressive-disclosure regime the 1.1
percent comes from. It cannot see the triggering failure mode at all, for
three structural reasons: it never models the decision to consult the menu, it
ends at ranking rather than at loading, and its null model is eight decoys
rather than a task distribution. The test's own docstring says as much, so the
2026-09-26 line's premise was right.

What it does measure, run this run on nine skills and 67 cases
(`python3 skills/_validation/trigger_test.py`): 58 of 67 pass, reliability
0.866, 95 percent interval [0.760, 0.937]. Restricted to the 49 cases that
name a skill, 43 are correct, 4 pick the wrong library skill and 2 pick a
decoy.

**Recall by the skill the case expects, which is not how the report prints it:**

| expected skill | correct | rate |
|---|---|---|
| harness-engineering | 6/11 | 55% |
| recursive-harness-self-improvement | 5/6 | 83% |
| the other seven skills | 32/32 | 100% |

The library's headline 0.866 is an average over a perfect seven, a near-perfect
neighbour, and one skill at little better than a coin flip. **Five of the six
failures expect `harness-engineering`; the sixth expects its nearest
neighbour.**

**And four of those five test content the skill deliberately no longer
carries.** Its own provenance records the 2026-09-30 ADR-38 rewrite: 164 lines
to under 120, "cut the order-of-operations section entire (action interface,
feedback enrichment, feedback consistency)" and "cut the localisation half of
the debugging section", because the bare subject supplied both unprompted.
Cases `ac-conf-2`, `cwe-conf-1` and `rhsi-conf-1` ask it to win prompts about
localising which sub-agent is at fault; `sle-conf-1` asks it to win a prompt
about tools versus turn framing versus loop structure. On two of those it
scores 0.0000 and 0.0534 -- no meaningful overlap, because the words are gone
from the description and the content is gone from the file. The cases were
authored 2026-09-22, 2026-09-26, 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30, all on or before
the day of the cut, and have been reported as failures for 32 days.

**Exactly one of the five is a real miss.** `he-pos-1` describes delta 3
almost verbatim -- forty one-line prompt fixes about to be pasted onto the end
of a system prompt -- and `harness-engineering` ranks fifth at 0.1217 behind
`agent-security-measurement` at 0.1671. Delta 3 is live, surviving content.
Its description is 124 words, the second shortest of nine against a library
mean of 138.6 and a cap of 150, so this is a coverage gap and not the
verbosity failure the engine is known to reward.

**The instrument defect underneath, and it is the generalizable half.** A
confusion case lives in the asking skill's `triggers.json` and names a
*different* skill as the expected answer, and the report aggregates by the
asking skill. So four of the five failures are printed under other skills'
suites, and a 55 percent skill is invisible in a page of per-suite numbers. A
revision to the named skill never sees the cases that depend on it: the
cross-file reference has no owner on the side that can break it. This is the
company register's L-A88 shape -- a summary whose key hides the class that
dominates -- applied to a test report rather than a dashboard.

## 7. Signal read

**The live check this seat owes every run (L-R1), against the web.** Third-party
Claude Code plugin marketplaces are shipping installed skill libraries in the
hundreds -- one public marketplace advertises 380 skills in a single plugin --
and a `find-skills` capability for discovering and installing skills on demand
is now part of the surface. Two things cross-reference against sections 2 and
3. A 380-skill installed library sits past the degradation onset of the only
size curve in reach by more than a factor of ten and past Skill-Use's 79 by
nearly five. And `find-skills` is a routing layer appearing in the ecosystem,
which is the thing *The Router Within* argues the menu cannot do.

**Steering.** The world is shipping libraries at 380 skills. The evidence says
selection accuracy falls off past about 30 on a synthetic library with 4o-generation
backbones, and triggering spreads from 0.324 to 0.972 at 79 real skills under
two real harnesses. **The corpus is thin on the join of the two**: nobody in
reach measures a real library above 50 skills under a real harness with a
current model. Ingest there, and it is the sharpest ingestion target this run
found.

**A positioning fact that falls out of it, for the PM and market seats.** Nine
skills is inside the plateau of the only published size curve. A competitor
shipping 380 is not. That is a selling point with a citation, and it is the
first one this shelf has had.

## 8. What looked worth ranking and is not

- **The search-engine synthesis of the size literature.** A live search
  returned a confident size grid -- 16-32 skills at 96-98 percent, 64 at 92,
  128 at 78, 256 at 64 -- attributed to `arxiv:2601.04748`. The paper's own
  section 5.2 sweeps a different grid and reports about 20 percent at 200.
  None of those four figures is in it. Not ranked, and recorded as an incident,
  because the surface is new: a search summary, not a seat and not a reference
  list.
- **"Gains trend downwards on stronger models" for `arxiv:2606.06923`.** The
  same synthesis asserted a monotone trend. Ordered by baseline, the deltas run
  +2.1, +5.3, 0.0, +0.8, +2.2. That is not monotone. The paper may support the
  claim with a fitted statistic; nothing should quote the trend until someone
  reads the section that states it.
- **`arxiv:2606.15390`, Not All Skills Help.** Looked like the findings-versus-procedure
  answer from its title. It is per-skill causal attribution by randomized
  masking and does not distinguish content kinds. Real paper, wrong question,
  and its causal-heterogeneity result is worth a later read on its own terms.
