## 1. What is rising, ranked by evidence rather than by date

**1. The benchmarks under the containment shelf are known-broken, and the fix
is published.** `arxiv:2510.05244` names two defects in AgentDojo — injection
vectors that overwrite task-critical content, so a utility drop can be data
deletion rather than agentic failure, and utility checks that use strict
cardinality, so a correct completion plus any side effect scores zero — and one
in Agent Security Bench, which forcibly inserts attacker-controlled tools into
every sub-task's tool subset and then counts invoking them as attack success.
Repairing the first two raised utility-under-attack by more than eighteen
points. This outranks everything else here because it attaches to CaMeL's
numbers, Fides' numbers, Progent's numbers and the two numbers this library
sells, all at once.

**2. CaMeL is the most-built-upon artifact in this territory and the library
quotes it through a confound.** `arxiv:2503.18813` is March 2025 and it is the
baseline that `arxiv:2510.05244`, the EvoSafeHarness line, and both of our new
skills define themselves against — which is the traction that earns it the
slot, not its date. Its AgentDojo utility is **35.1% to 77.3% depending on the
backbone**, its own headline is 77% against 84% undefended, and a third party
reports 53.60 on gpt-4o. `skills/agent-security-measurement/SKILL.md:85` says
"twice the utility of a fixed capability-based defence at the same zero-success
operating point." Twice of 82.8 is 41.4, which is CaMeL on Gemini 2.5 Pro.
Against CaMeL's best backbone the ratio is 1.07.

**3. The output side of the tool boundary carries the leverage, found twice
from different literatures.** `arxiv:2510.05244`: a Tool-Output Firewall that
regenerates the tool result without the injection reaches 0.00 attack success
at 74.09 benign utility, against a Tool-Input Firewall that redacts arguments
at 18.15 and 70.01. `arxiv:2601.04688`, measuring utility and not security at
all: removing a Hoare postcondition check costs more than removing the
precondition check — GPT-5.2 MCP-Avg 57.0 full, 52.5 without the precondition,
46.2 without the postcondition. Two instruments, two metrics, one shape. **This
is the week's candidate pattern.**

**4. Adaptive attack is the difference between 0.00 and 30.0 and the field is
only now standardising it.** The same paper's three-stage cascade — existing
attacks, then seven second-order semantic templates that drop instruction-like
surface forms, then a defence-aware attacker refining over ten iterations —
takes its own firewall from 0.00 to **30.0** cumulative attack success on
AgentDojo's Slack suite, and takes an LLM detector from 8.0 to **75.5**. The
paper's abstract says "perfect security." Its section 8 says 30.0.

**5. Least privilege generated before untrusted content arrives is most of the
defence.** `arxiv:2504.11703` reports 39.9% → **2.5%** attack success from the
initial LLM-generated policy alone with updates disabled, and 1.0% with the
full update mechanism, at maintained utility. The mechanism worth copying is
**monotonic confinement**: an SMT solver classifies each policy update as a
narrowing or an expansion, narrowings apply automatically, expansions need
approval, and **only 6% of updates turn out to be expansions**. Manual approval
reaches 0%.

### The find of this pass, and the live-web check is what found it

**`arxiv:2610.02858`, Harness-Aware Distillation for Small Language Model
Agents** (Choi, Moon, Nam, Lee; KAIST AI; 02 Oct 2026). Not in any reference
list this seat has read. It came out of the ecosystem-events check L-R1 requires
every run, which is the clearest return that check has produced here.

It measures the mechanism under `skills/harness-engineering`'s delta 1 on
evidence independent of everything that delta rests on: a different group, three
different benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop, ScienceWorld), and student-teacher pairs
at 1.7B from 8B and 0.6B from 30B rather than two 26-to-30B models from Gemini.
Its Table 11 scores harness utilization as the unweighted mean of five
task-agnostic rules on ALFWorld. The untrained student scores 65.7 and one
distillation baseline comes in at 59.2, below it. On the single rule that matters
most to the delta, "do not repeat an action the harness reported as failed", the
untrained student scores 61.2 and **three of the four distillation baselines fall
below it**, the worst at 28.6. On the stall rule, untrained 18.1 against 17.6,
11.0 and 5.3.

So a second group, at a tenth the model scale, measures imitation of a stronger
teacher degrading a student's use of the harness's own failure reports below
doing nothing at all. That is correction 7's finding arrived at independently,
and it is the strongest support delta 1 has ever had.

What it does not do is close the open queue line, and the reason is precise:
HAD's harness is fixed and generic rather than evolved for the student, and its
supervision is a teacher-action contrast rather than off-policy imitation of a
whole trajectory. Those two variables are exactly what `2609.09134`'s result
turns on. Evidence for the mechanism, not a replication.

The convergence is the part a builder can use. Both groups independently repair
an imitation failure under a harness with an on-policy contrastive signal built
from the student's own rollouts: in-place correction of the single failing turn
in one, an action preference scored after the student's own reasoning in the
other, which reaches 63.43 on unseen ALFWorld against 47.01 for the best baseline
and beats its own 8B teacher at 51.49. The skill prescribes the first and has
never heard of the second.

**Extraction target for the skill seat, ranked above the three corrections
above**: the claim cluster is `2609.09134` plus `2610.02858` plus `2607.08938`,
and what it supports is a claim no single paper states — teacher imitation under
a harness transfers knowledge and loses harness fit, and the repair is on-policy
and contrastive at both scales measured.
