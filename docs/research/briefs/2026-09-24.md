## The finding that reorders the rest

Every claim alexandria holds came from one feed.

7,991 papers are ingested. 151 of them produced all 693 claims, and all
151 arrived through `hf-daily`, HuggingFace's daily curator picks, tier
`b`. The triage model has judged 290 papers in the pipeline's life and
every one was tier `b`. Tier `a`, the seven arXiv categories in
`sources.yaml`, has 2,329 papers waiting and has never had a single
paper judged. Tier `a-low` has 936 and not one triage row of any kind.
The nineteen RSS feeds have contributed nothing.

So `sources.yaml` does not currently curate anything. It is a list of
places we fetch from, and one entry on it is the corpus. Every source
proposal this seat has made, including last week's, changed what we
collect and nothing about what we know.

The cost is legible against the owner's own orders. Her named
priorities, with the corpus behind each:

| named priority | ordered | claims in corpus |
|---|---|---|
| Agent containment (L4, top priority) | 2026-09-19 | **0** |
| Agent containerization (L4) | 2026-09-19 | **0** |
| Synthetic data and self-play (L3b) | 2026-09-23 | **0** |
| Decision frameworks, OODA and kin (L4) | 2026-09-19 | 1 |
| Reasoning models at the frontier (L3a) | 2026-09-23 | 5, none of them about reasoning training |
| Orchestration for real (L4) | 2026-09-19 | 8 |

Against that, inference and serving has 74 claims and retrieval and
memory 51. Nobody chose that shape. It is the shape of HuggingFace's
daily picks, and it is what the corpus will keep looking like however
carefully this seat curates, because 96% of what we fetch never reaches
a judgment.

Containment is the sharpest case. It was made the top research priority
five days ago, and the corpus holds zero claims that mention a sandbox,
an escape, isolation, least privilege, or prompt injection. Containment
research lives in cs.CR, cs.CR is tier `a-low`, and `a-low` is the tier
with 936 papers and zero triage rows. The priority is not underserved by
judgment. It is unreachable by construction.

The fix is not mine and not a source diff. It is engineer-lane and it
is already written: `pipeline/triage.py` gained interleaved per-tier
draining on 2026-09-19 (commits 74e0c99, 73da628), for exactly this.
It has never run. See "What the engineer should know" below.

forbids.

## What the engineer should know

Three things, none of them mine to fix, all of them blocking product
quality before the 2026-10-13 launch.

**1. Nothing merged since about 2026-09-12 runs in production, code or
prompt.** Incident 25 filed this as a prompt problem. It is broader.
`prompts/triage.md` at HEAD matches the sha production recorded, which
dates the last deploy to about 2026-09-12. `prompts/interpret.md` was
sharpened on 2026-09-19 and every edge written since, through
2026-09-23, still carries the 2026-09-07 prompt's sha. And
`pipeline/triage.py`'s tier-interleaving fix, merged 2026-09-19 to end
exactly the starvation described at the top of this brief, has not
changed a single day's triage: tier `b` only on 09-20, 09-21, 09-22 and
09-23. A CI check comparing HEAD's shas against the newest recorded
`method` and `prompt_sha` would have failed every day for five days.

**2. The interpret worker is rate-limited, not under-sized.**
`MAX_CLAIMS_PER_RUN` is 100 and the run selects 100. It processes about
eleven and then takes a 429 from Groq, and `break` leaves the rest
queued (interpret.py:93-97). Inflow is about 40 claims a day. The queue
grows about 29 a day, stands at 460, and has never shrunk. At this rate
the graph will still be describing 2026-09-12 on launch day, with a
backlog near 1,000 and every launch-week digest item carrying no
claim-graph evidence at all. That is O1 KR3 failing on the date, and it
is the cheapest possible thing to notice late.

**3. Incident 24 has one cause, not two, and the open question is
answered.** The incident records it as unresolved whether the Modal
Monday cron fired on 2026-09-21. It fired. `citation_log` holds 62 rows
stamped 2026-09-21 15:00:00 UTC, the cron slot exactly, matching the
15:00:00 stamp of the 2026-09-14 run that did publish. `weekly()` calls
`check_citations` first and `write_digest` after, so the run reached
Neon, wrote its citation rows, and died at the model call. The
groq/compound 404 is the whole story and nobody needs the Modal
dashboard's schedule history.
