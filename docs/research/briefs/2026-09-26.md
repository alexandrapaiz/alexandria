# Curation brief — 2026-09-26

Research agent, weekly run. Owner directive this dispatch: make sure the
library includes research on reasoning models.

The short version. The owner is right that the corpus is thin on reasoning
models and right about every remedy she named, and the reason she gave for
the thinness is not what the log shows. The rubric is not suppressing
reasoning work. When the live rubric actually reads a reasoning paper it
sends 58% of them to distill, against 4.1% for the corpus as a whole. The
reasoning gap is the general intake gap wearing a specific face: 185 of our
209 reasoning-titled papers have never been judged by the rubric at all.
So this run shipped her remedies anyway, because they have to be in place
before the backlog drains, and it hands the engineer the number that
actually governs.

## 2. The number that governs all of this

Real triage throughput is **20 papers a day**: 10 tier `a` and 10 tier `b`, one
batch each. Everything else in the log is the backfill rule.

`triage_queue` holds 4,973 papers. By tier: a 2,645, a-low 1,179, d 835, c 177,
b 137. arXiv alone is 3,824 of them, spanning 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-24.

At 10 tier-`a` papers a day against 2,645 queued and roughly 130 arriving
daily, the queue does not drain. It grows.

Tier `a-low` received **zero** triage rows in the last four days and holds
1,179 papers. cs.LG lives in `a-low`. The owner's directive names cs.LG as a
place reasoning research lands. Right now nothing in it is reachable.

Credit where it is due: the interleaved per-tier draining that last Thursday's
brief reported as written-but-never-run (commits 74e0c99, 73da628) **has now
run**. arXiv received its first triage rows ever on 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25,
10 a day. The fix works. The budget behind it is two batches out of the
twenty-five the function plans for, because the Groq free tier stops the run
("rate limited by Groq; stopping — next run resumes"). That is the constraint,
and the engineer's Kimi migration is the thing that lifts it.

This is why the rubric text shipped today rather than after the unblock. When
capacity arrives, 3,824 arXiv papers get judged in a burst. Either the rubric

## 4. The digest, 2026-W39

The judgment holds and one section should not have shipped.

Verified against the corpus: the Harness-Zero numbers are exact. Claim 668
carries 23.3% base, 44.3% distilled, 41.7% with the harness attached; claim 669
carries the 82.3% pattern recovery over 28 patterns; claim 670 carries the 30%
versus 3-15% supervision ablation. The issue reports them correctly and the

**The error is the closing section.** "The earlier claim that 'only a few
GPU-hours' were needed now looks like unnecessary work" rests on one edge,
190 `contradicts` 188, and both claims come from the same paper
(`arxiv:2609.10522`, Show-Harness). A paper does not contradict itself. Claim
188 says Show-Harness makes small-VLM adaptation cheap; claim 190 says its
semantic interface removes the need for extra pretraining. Those are two
findings of one result, at most a refinement. Claim 188 was deprecated on the
strength of that edge and the deprecation was then published to readers as a
thing the field is leaving behind. Mis-typed edge, wrongful deprecation,
published over-claim, in that order.

**The graph has five `contradicts` edges and three are wrong.**

- 190 -> 188: same paper, as above. Should be `refines` or `supports`.
- 12 -> 11: an expert implementation scoring 82.2% against Claude Opus 5 under
  Claude Code scoring 23.9% on the same benchmark. Two systems, one benchmark,
  different scores. That is a comparison.
- 85 -> 12: the same approach scoring 12.5% on four memory-dependent RMBench
  tasks against 82.2% on the full benchmark. A harder subset, same method.
  That is a scope refinement.
- 82 -> 5 and 136 -> 129 look defensible.

Three out of five is a pattern, not an anecdote, and the charter points it at
`prompts/interpret.md`. I am not proposing that diff, for the reason in section 5.

## 7. Radar note for the writer

Three candidates for the next issue, ordered by what the evidence supports
rather than by date.

**Spending thinking time only where it pays.** The strongest reasoning story we
can tell with our own receipts, and it has the rare shape of a result plus its
own counter-example. When2Think reaches 40.0% Pass@3 on AIME25 by learning when
a question needs a long answer and when it does not, beating both always-compress
and always-route baselines (claim 511, arxiv:2609.19671). Against it, claim 700
(arxiv:2609.25804): on a judgment task rather than a maths task, turning the
reasoning budget from lowest to highest moved one model by -0.2 points and
another by +2.2, which is noise. The honest line for a builder is that longer
thinking pays on verifiable problems and is a coin-flip on taste, and both
halves are ours, measured.

**Reasoning traces are mostly redundant for post-training.** "Revisiting
Complete Reasoning Traces for Post-Training" is distilled and in hand. If the
numbers survive a read, this is the cheapest actionable result of the month for
anyone fine-tuning: stop paying to collect full traces.

**One correction owed.** 2026-W39's closing section retired a claim that was
never contradicted (section 4). If the writer runs a standing retractions line, this
is its first entry; if not, the deprecation should simply not be repeated.

Not yet: anything framed as "the field moved to reasoning this month". We hold
