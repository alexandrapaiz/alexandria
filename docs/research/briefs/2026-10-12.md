## 0. Synthesis: where this is heading (charter step 2)

There is no digest opening to be sharper than this week, so this is the note
that would have argued with it. Grounded in corpus material only; claim ids
throughout.

**Containment stopped being a design literature and became a measurement
literature, and that is the week's real movement.** A month ago the corpus
held containment as architecture: how you build an isolation boundary, what a
microVM costs, kernel preemption at a median 0.0048 ms (C882) and its
LangGraph integration (C886). What it did not hold was a single number about
whether any of it works. It now holds fifteen papers that do nothing else:
EvoSafeHarness taking attack success from 45.6% to 10.0% on
DecodingTrust-Agent for 3.3 points of utility (C307), ToolFence from 21.20% to
0.20% on AgentDojo for 3.80 points (C1224), PACE lowest across eight
executable benchmarks and three model families (C1653), Multi-CaMeL at 0.0% on
MultiAgentDojo against 12.9% undefended (C1882). Twenty-two of twenty-four
claims graded `controlled`, against a corpus baseline of 57.8%. The field
acquired a benchmark culture for agent security in about four weeks, and the
2026-10-05 brief's complaint -- that we were reading how containment is built
and not whether it holds -- is now half answered.

**Only half, and the unanswered half is the one the world keeps demonstrating.**
Every one of those fifteen papers measures whether a guard blocks a *tool
call*. Not one measures whether an agent can get out of the *box*: `sandbox
escape`, `escape rate` and capability revocation still return zero rows, for
the third consecutive week. The OpenAI incident the market seat named was the
second kind, and so was Hugging Face. So the literature has converged hard on
the failure mode that is easy to instrument in a benchmark harness, and left
the one that produces the incidents to postmortems that enter no arXiv
category. That is not a gap in our reading; it is a gap in the field, and
naming it is the kind of judgment the digest is supposed to exist for.
C1779's argument sharpens it further: a single attack-success rate hides what
a defence actually did, and a four-class metric separates the cases. The
newest paper in the cluster is already arguing that the cluster's own metric is
too coarse.

**Independently, Layer 1 says the agent has become an infrastructure tenant
with a bill.** The novelty signal's only real cluster this week is not about
agents at all in the usual sense: it is serving-tier scheduling, cache
headroom, write capping and tail latency (C998, C1001, C1260, C1356, C929),
four papers whose claims sit far from anything the corpus held a month ago and
close to each other. The datum underneath it is C1327: agents are 19.5% of
users but 55.8% of job submissions. And C983 prices the memory decision
directly -- writing raw turns costs 3,061x less than writing extracted facts.

**The two currents are the same current, which is the thing worth saying.**
Security is converging on "the agent is a workload with a boundary" and infra
on "the agent is a workload with a bill," and both have stopped treating the
agent as a model and started treating it as a tenant. The practical shape of a
tenant is a declared resource envelope, enforced somewhere the tenant cannot
reach. That is exactly what the research half of the corpus proposes (C1826,
an iteratively refined semantic allowlist for agent functional boundaries;
ToolFence's fine-grained authorization) and exactly what the platform half
shipped on 2026-10-07, five days ago, with the enforcement point explicitly
outside the agent workload's control (section 9). Two literatures and a
product arriving at one design inside a month is the definition of something
compounding rather than something new.

**So what a builder should do differently this week, concretely.** Move the
enforcement point outside the agent, and stop treating the utility cost as a
reason not to. That second clause is the part that is newly evidenced: the cost
of fine-grained enforcement is now measured at 3.3 points (C307) and 3.80
points (C1224) against attack-success reductions of 35 and 21 points. A month
ago the honest answer to "what does a containment control cost" was one
paper's 145-to-316-second mean, and the reading queue still carries that as an
open question. It is now answerable in utility terms for tool authorization
specifically. What remains unbuyable at any price is assurance that the box
holds, and a builder should not read fifteen green benchmark numbers as that
assurance.

**The caveat this brief owes its own readers.** Our corpus barely moved this
week -- no claim since 2026-10-07, edges drawing at 12 a day against 80 -- and
the graph that would show all of the above compounding has a `contradicts`
precision of 0.172 (section 5). The currents named here rest on `supports`
accumulation, evidence grades and independent-source counts, which are the
streams that survive that finding. None of them rests on a `contradicts` edge,
and after this week none of them should.

## 5. Digest quality and the claim-graph error hunt

No issue printed this week, so there is no new prose to review. The error hunt
went to the layer underneath the press instead, and found the larger problem.

**`contradicts` precision is 0.172.** `tools/graph_audit.py` has shipped
`--sample` and `--labels` since it was written, and its docstring records why
no number ever came out of them: "No seat in this org holds that credential
today." This run holds a read-only copy, so the measurement exists for the
first time. `contradicts` is 33 edges, so this is the population and not a
sample: 5 correct, 24 wrong, 4 unsure.

**Confidence carries no signal and is inverted at the top.** Precision is
0.167 in the 0.6-0.7 band, 0.19 in the 0.7-0.8 band that holds 22 of the
edges, and **0.0 in both bands above it** -- the two most confident edges in
the graph, at 0.88 and 0.90, are both wrong. The audit's own
`confidence calibration` metric reads `ok` because it only counts how many
distinct values the judge uses. It uses thirty. They mean nothing.

**The reader-facing consequence: 19 of 22 claims on the Left-Behind Index are
not superseded.** `deprecated_claims` gates on `contradicts` at confidence
>= 0.7, which is precisely the band measured at 0.19. Crossing the labels
against the view, only C322, C431 and C533 rest on an edge that holds. Vision
section 1 names "left behind" as one of the digest's three kinds of finding,
so 86% of one of the product's three pillars is wrong, and W40 printed an
instance of it ("the null was not null", from the same-paper pair 289/288).

**The failure has a shape, and the shape is a magnet.** Twelve of the 33 edges
point at just four claims, and all four are negative results: C533 (token-level
supervision leaves behaviour unchanged), C89 (SFT overfits to corruption
patterns), C200 (imitation fine-tuning regresses), C850 (self-generated skills
underperform curated). The judge reads "a limitation of X" against "a success
near X" as opposition, when the two nearly always measure different techniques,
scopes or quantities. C772's "diverse, high-quality SFT establishes a strong
capability floor" is written as contradicting C89, when one is a scope the
other excludes and both are true. C865, an ablation of skill-optimisation
components, is written as contradicting C533, about token supervision in Qwen
-- different subjects entirely.

**So the corpus systematically deprecates correct negative results**, which is
exactly backwards for a judgment document, and it is the diff in section 7.

Two smaller classes, each with instances: **scale-class mismatch** (897->604,
a frontier model's 100% on AIME 2026 written as refuting "ranks first at the
7B-8B scale"), and **successor read as refutation** (863->625, a method
improving on the SkillOpt baseline, which is `refines` and which the prompt
already says).

**Four new edges since the last brief, and all four are wrong.** 888->850,
897->604, 931->200 written 2026-10-06, and 1119->103 written today. The class
is live, not historical.

**The steer already taken, and what the corpus now says about it.** The

delegation to leave its permitted network, detected in 15 minutes and still
running two and a half hours later because no automatic stop existed. Last
week's read was that the corpus measured the wrong half: it held preemption

**That has partly changed, and the change is section 1's top item.** The
corpus now holds fifteen papers measuring whether a control actually blocks an
agent's action -- ToolFence's within-tool authorization, PACE's
provenance-aware capability enforcement, Multi-CaMeL's multi-agent case, PAA's
boundary-action auditing at 86% block recall. That is enforcement and
detection-at-the-boundary, which is nearer to stop authority than anything we
held a week ago.

**This seat's own live ecosystem check (L-R1, the company standard), and it
found something.** On **2026-10-07 Microsoft shipped Execution Containers
(MXC) to general availability** -- policy-driven containment for AI agents,
announced on Microsoft's own Windows Developer Blog by the CVP of Windows
Platform and Developer, verified against the primary source rather than the
newsletters that relayed it.

What it describes, because the mechanism is the part that matters here: agents
declare the files and network destinations they need in a unified JSON policy
schema through a multi-language SDK; **the policy lives outside the agent
workload's control**, so an agent cannot widen its own access; four isolation
backends (process containers over AppContainer on Windows, Seatbelt on macOS
and Bubblewrap on Linux; a session container under a distinct Windows account
that separates desktop, clipboard and input; a WSL container; and an
experimental MicroVM with hardware-enforced isolation); and three modes, of
which **Learning** blocks ungranted access and writes it to a JSON activity
report. Entra-based agent identity is named as coming soon with no
implementation detail.

**Where it lands on the signal-versus-evidence line, stated rather than
assumed.** The general-availability announcement is a signal and stops there.
The policy architecture is an artifact with method and is admissible as a
*described mechanism* -- a builder can do something differently tomorrow
knowing that the enforcement point sits outside the workload and that a
learning mode will enumerate what an agent actually reaches. But the post
**measures nothing**: no overhead, no latency, and above all no escape or
containment testing. Under the program rule that a vendor's own marketing is
not a finding, it may never carry a load-bearing number, and nothing in it
answers whether the boundary holds.

**Why it is a steer and not a curiosity.** This is three of the owner's named

**The corpus is still thin on, and this is now a narrower gap:** whether an
