# 2026-W39's opening sentence: the ruling, and the words to set

Writer run 61, 2026-11-01. `check_voice.py standalone` fails on
`site/content/issues/2026-W39.md:6` and on the live page un-rendered from
`libraryofalexandria.dev/library/2026-W39`. Both serve this sentence:

> You have spent the week watching agents get faster by thinking less at test
> time.

This file is the decision and the exact words. It is not set by this seat.
`site/` is the frontend seat's surface and that has not changed.

## Why this is a finding today and not five weeks ago

It was a finding five weeks ago. Nothing opened a row for it.

Canon law 13 quotes the owner rejecting this sentence's near twin by name, in
her words, "dont assume readers read each issue", and the law then widens
itself to the grammar so it can be checked in one pass: "**the opening never
says what the reader has done, read, watched, noticed or felt** ... The shape
is a subject and a verb and nothing subtler: 'you' with a past-tense verb,
anywhere in the opening. There is no version of it this law allows and no
material that earns an exception."

So the law's own text carries the specimen, the grammar, the owner's words and
the repair, and the sentence it was written about is still the first thing a
stranger reads on that page. The gate that catches it is this seat's and has
been catching it; `awaiting-her-word.md` had no row, because the two commands
that re-confirm this register are `reading` and `claims` and neither of them
reads an opening. A register is only as honest as the scope of the commands
that re-confirm it, which is a sentence that register already says about
itself.

One more reason it stayed invisible: `standalone --live` raised `ValueError`
instead of printing the hit, because `path.relative_to(ROOT)` cannot name a
served edition fetched into `/tmp`. That is repaired in this run's diff. The
pass printed a clean archive without complaint and crashed only once it had
something to say, so a run that pointed it at the product saw a traceback
rather than this sentence.

## The ruling: replace the first sentence, and carry no notice

**The words, verbatim, and they are not this seat's invention.** Canon law 13
already contains the repair, written into the law when the owner's dispatch
widened it. The first sentence becomes:

> Agents have been getting cheaper by thinking less at the moment they answer.

Nothing else in the paragraph moves. The rest already works, because the
sentences after it are about the field and about what a builder can do:

> That trick has a ceiling: eventually the scaffolding that steers them
> becomes the thing that slows them down. This week the field went after the
> scaffolding itself, and one result suggests you can train it in and then
> leave it behind.

The thread survives. It is stated rather than charged to the reader, which is
the whole of law 13's repair, and the second person that remains is in the
present and the future where the law puts it.

**Why "at the moment they answer" rather than "at test time".** The law's
repair makes that swap and it is the outsider test doing the work: "test time"
is the field's name for the moment a model answers a request, and a stranger
reads it as a testing phase. The replacement costs four words and needs no
gloss.

## No notice on this page, and that is a decision rather than an omission

The 2026-W42 correction of 2026-10-26 carries a notice, and this one does not.
The two cases differ on the one axis that matters.

That ruling pulled a section whose three statements about overturned findings
did not hold against the papers it cited. A reader who kept the email and
opened the page would find research missing, so the page owed them a sentence
saying what went and why. Canon law 7's first clause asks for exactly that: a
limitation disclosed in one plain sentence where it materially affects the
day's content.

Nothing about this day's content changes here. No finding is added, removed,
or restated. One sentence about the reader becomes a sentence about the field,
and every claim the issue makes about anybody's research stands untouched. A
notice would be narrating an edit that changed no fact, which is the method
talking about itself, and that is the half of law 7 that bans narration.

**And the divergence-from-the-send objection, which is real and does not
carry.** The W42 ruling held that the correct archive is a subset of what was
sent rather than a rewrite of it, and a replaced sentence is a rewrite. It
also held why: a subscriber received that issue and the email cannot be
recalled. The difference is who reads the page now. The people the send was
true for had read the issue before it; the people reading
`/library/2026-W39` in November have not, and law 13 exists because the
archive is read out of order by strangers. The sentence is unverifiable for
every one of them. Leaving it to preserve fidelity to an email sent five weeks
ago keeps a false sentence in front of the only audience the page still has.

By the same test the two semicolons that ruling declined to fix stay unfixed
here too, and so does everything else on the page. This is one sentence.

## What this does not touch

`pipeline/weekly.py`'s `MASTHEAD` is unchanged and still recites the framework
slots in plain-English synonyms on every issue the press sends, which is a
canon law 12 violation filed for 29 days and the engineer's. The live
`2026-W40` page prints three headings of the shape law 12 forbids outright,
"What is gaining ground: ...", "What is genuinely new: ...", "What fell
behind: ...", and `check_voice.py measure` reports all three on every run. Both
are named in this run's review and neither is repaired here, because this file
is one sentence on one page and bundling them would hold the cheap fix behind
the expensive ones.

`site/content/issues/2026-W39.md`, line 6, first sentence only. The live page
