# Site copy, drafted for the frontend seat

Owner's order, 2026-09-19, recorded in docs/voice/taste.md and
docs/design/taste.md: the voice canon now governs site copy, not only the
digest. She rejected the library page's prose and ordered the mission
page rewritten under the same rules, guided by the mission line and the
OKR seat's purpose framing.

**The division of labour.** The writer seat drafts the words. The
frontend seat sets them, owns the markup, and decides nothing about the
prose. Two files are affected: `site/app/library/page.jsx` and
`site/app/mission/page.jsx`. Nothing here is a layout instruction.

**What every line below was checked against.** The ten laws in
docs/voice/canon.md, the tells in docs/voice/ban-list.md, and the two
rulings that bind hardest here: the outsider test (a builder from
another team understands it whole, with no club words) and law 3, sell
the product and never the recipe. The design register adds one more:
headings never carry explanatory subtitles, and the mission line stands
alone and untouched.

---

## 1. The library page

### What is there now, and why she rejected it

```
Every issue, in full.
The weekly digest is free to read and nothing in it is held back.
Start with the most recent issue.
```

Four things are wrong with it, and only the first is about taste.

**It says "weekly", and the product is daily.** An issue lands every
day, and Monday's is the one that takes the week as a whole. Copy that
calls the whole thing a weekly digest describes six sevenths of the
product out of existence.

**It answers a question the visitor has not asked yet.** "Nothing in it
is held back" is about the business model. A person who has just arrived
does not yet know what an issue contains, so telling them the paywall is
absent means nothing to them. Reassurance before description is
back to front, and it makes the page sound defensive.

**The second line repeats the first.** "In full" and "nothing in it is
held back" are one idea wearing two coats. The intro spends its whole
length restating the heading instead of adding the next fact (ban list
7).

**"Start with the most recent issue" is an instruction, not a welcome.**
It tells a reader to do the one thing they were already going to do.

### Candidate A, and this is my pick

```
A new issue every day.

Each one is a short read on what changed in AI research and what it
means for what you are building. Monday's is longer and puts the whole
week together. All of them are free, and they stay up.
```

### Candidate B

```
Today's issue, and every one before it.

The library reads new AI research every day and writes up what actually
changed, in plain words. Monday's issue steps back and takes the week at
once. Free to read, all the way back.
```

### Why A

Both pass the outsider test and both say daily without arguing about it.
A wins on one practical fact: **on the pilot Monday there will be
exactly one issue on the page.** Her ruling of 2026-09-19 retires
2026-W37 and makes Monday's the first the public reads, so B's heading,
"and every one before it", is false on the day it ships and stays thin
for a week after. A is true on day one and truer every day after that.
B's heading also promises an archive in the same breath it promises
recency, which is two ideas in a line that should carry one.

A's third sentence does the work the rejected line was reaching for.
"All of them are free, and they stay up" is the same promise as "nothing
is held back", except it is offered rather than defended, and "they stay
up" adds the fact the old copy never said: the archive does not expire.

Hold B in reserve for the point where the archive is genuinely deep,
around fifty issues, when "every one before it" becomes the stronger
claim of the two.

### Two notes for the frontend seat

**Do not set the daily copy before the daily ships.** The daily cadence
is in PR #35 and unmerged at the time of writing. Until that merges, an
issue lands on Mondays only, and candidate A would be a promise the
product does not keep. Set it in the same merge window, not before.

**The empty state below it stays as it is.** "The first issue is on its
way. Check back on Monday." is correct, warm, and reads cleanly under
either candidate.

## 2. The mission page

### The constraint

The H1 is exactly `Accelerate every builder to frontier speed.` It
stands alone, untouched, with no subtitle under it, per the standing
design law. Everything below replaces the five paragraphs inside
`<div className="prose">`.

### What is wrong with the current prose

**It sells the recipe.** "It does so without a person in the loop", "it
proposes improvements to its own prompts and to the pipeline that runs
them". Autonomy is how alexandria is built, and to a visitor deciding
whether to trust it, "no person checks this" reads as a warning rather
than a feature. Law 3 is explicit, and the closing paragraph is the
clearest violation on the site.

**It is stale.** "Once a week the library writes the digest" is no
longer true.

**Why never arrives before what.** The page opens on volume, moves
immediately to mechanism, and never says plainly what a builder gets
out of it. The mission line above it promises speed, and the prose
under it never picks the word back up.

**Club words the page hands over without introduction.** "Prompts" and
"pipeline" are machinery words, and an outsider has no reason to hold
them.

**The end state is missing.** The OKR file's purpose framing says the
north star is product quality measured against real competitors and the
goal is frontier research put to direct use, compounding and
self-maintaining. None of that reaches the page today.

### The rewrite

> The frontier is not a secret. It is a few thousand papers a week, and
> you are never going to read them.
>
> So most teams work from what filtered through: a headline, a thread, a
> colleague's summary of a result that was quietly overturned a month
> ago. The distance between a real finding and something you can use is
> where the speed goes.
>
> alexandria closes that distance. It reads the new research every day
> and writes up what actually changed, in plain words, for people who
> build software rather than people who write papers. Monday's issue
> takes the week as a whole.
>
> Under the writing is the part that makes it hold. Every paper is
> broken into claims, meaning one finding at a time, kept with the
> evidence behind it and the steps that produced it. Claims are linked
> to each other, so when new work supports one, sharpens it, or shows it
> was wrong, the record says so instead of leaving both versions in
> circulation.
>
> That same record is what your agents work from. Procedures that hold
> up become skills, which are files an agent loads to do the work, and
> each one stays tied to the claims behind it. When the research moves,
> the skill is revised or retired with the reason attached, and you do
> not have to be the one who noticed.
>
> The library keeps this up on its own, every day, because a record of a
> field moving this fast cannot wait for anyone to find the time. And it
> is held to one test: next to the best thing you could be reading
> instead, this should be the one that is right more often.
>
> **You read to decide. Your agents load to act.**

### How the rewrite answers the brief

**Why before what.** The first two paragraphs are the problem, stated
without naming alexandria. The product does not appear until the reader
already wants it.

**Frontier speed, picked up from the H1.** "The distance between a real
finding and something you can use is where the speed goes" is the
mission line's own claim in the reader's terms, and the heading stops
being a slogan floating above unrelated prose.

**Compounding and self-maintaining, in plain words.** Paragraph four is
compounding, paragraph five is self-maintaining. Neither word appears,
because both are abstractions the reader has to translate, and the
concrete version is shorter anyway: the record says so instead of
leaving both versions in circulation, and the skill is revised or
retired with the reason attached.

**The north star as a promise, not a process note.** "Next to the best
thing you could be reading instead, this should be the one that is right
more often" is quality against real competitors, said the way a reader
can hold the page to it.

**Autonomy without the recipe.** One clause, "keeps this up on its own,
every day", and a reason that serves the reader rather than the builder
of the system. Everything about prompts, pipelines and self-improvement
is gone. That work belongs in engineering writing, not on the page where
a stranger decides what this is.

**The dual audience.** Paragraph five carries it and the standing close
lands it, which satisfies the design ruling of 2026-09-18 that both
audiences appear across the surface rather than in one sentence on one
page.

**Outsider test.** One term of art is introduced, "claims", and it
carries its definition in the same sentence. "Skills" is introduced the
same way. Nothing else on the page needs the field's vocabulary. No
acronyms, no method names, no internal words.

**Density.** Seven paragraphs, two to three sentences each, one idea to
a sentence. Two short sentences carry the opening, which is where a
visitor decides whether to keep reading.

### Setting notes for the frontend seat

- The bold `<b>` treatment on `claims` and `agent skills` in the current
  markup can stay on `claims` and `skills` if the page wants it. The
  prose does not depend on it.
- The last line is the house's standing close and is never reworded.
- `&rsquo;` and friends: the copy above is written with straight
  apostrophes and plain ASCII hyphens on purpose (ban list 13). Curly
  quotes in body prose are the frontend seat's call as typography, but
  no non-breaking hyphens and no narrow spaces.

## What this draft does not cover

The home page's `about-inline` paragraph still opens "Every week the
library reads new AI research", which is the same stale cadence claim
the library page carries. It was not in her order, so it is not
rewritten here, but it will read as a contradiction the moment either
page above goes daily. One sentence, and the fix is the same fix.
