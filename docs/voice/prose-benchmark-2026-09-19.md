# Prose benchmark: alexandria against the majors

**Headline: we lose on every prose axis to every contender, and four of
the five gaps are heading-and-warmth problems the generator can fix
tonight.**

Writer seat, run 4, 2026-09-19. Built for the chair's deck: every
section carries a one-line headline and bullets a slide can lift
verbatim. Sources are the contenders' real issues from this week and
this month, read on the live web on 2026-09-19. Quotes are short and
attributed. alexandria is graded harshly on purpose, because a
flattering instrument is a broken one.

Read alongside: docs/voice/canon.md (the twelve laws), docs/voice/taste.md
(the owner's rulings), docs/voice/reviews/2026-09-19.md (runs 1 to 3).

## 2. Side by side, four moments in an issue

**Headline: put the openings next to each other and ours is the only
one that could have been generated.**

### 2a. How each one opens

- **Morning Brew**: "Ahoy, mateys! It's International Talk Like a Pirate
  Day, so here's a cheat sheet to consult on your landlubbing weekend:"
  (Sep 19, 2026)
- **The Batch**: "Dear friends, / The loudest voices stoking fears about
  AI dangers have made tremendous headway in the past two weeks."
  (issue 371)
- **Import AI**: "Welcome to Import AI, a newsletter about AI research.
  Import AI runs on arXiv, cappuccinos, and feedback from readers."
  (#472)
- **Money Stuff**: "I think sometimes, in our age of artificial
  intelligence, about two opposing theories of asset pricing."
  (Sep 9, 2026)
- **TLDR AI**: no opening at all. The subject line is three item names
  with emoji, and the first line of body is a sponsor.
- **alexandria, shipped**: "This week the most active ideas revolve
  around two themes." (2026-W37)
- **alexandria, intended**: the generator now demands a greeting that is
  "true about this particular day" and names its own failure case,
  "Welcome to another edition".

What the slide says:

- Three of five majors put a person in the first eight words: "Ahoy",
  "Dear friends", "I think sometimes".
- Import AI proves a fixed greeting can work, but only because it is a
  joke with a fact in it, and the items underneath are in first person.
- Ours announces a count of themes. **A sentence that starts "This week
  the most active ideas revolve around two themes" has told the reader
  nothing and has spent the best line in the issue doing it.**
- Money Stuff is the one to steal from: an idea, in first person, that
  the news then tests.

### 2b. How each one heads a section

- **Morning Brew**: "HEIR-MAN OF THE BOARD" over "Warren Buffett gets
  his son set, rides into the sunset".
- **The Batch**: "How To Secure Agents for the Masses".
- **Import AI**: "**DeepMind makes a swarm of agents to solve math
  problems; they start cheating and counter-cheating:** _...Emergence of
  specialized roles, cheaters and non-cheaters, and more within the
  swarm..._"
- **Money Stuff**: "AI credit trading".
- **TLDR AI**: "Headlines & Launches", "Deep Dives & Analysis",
  "Engineering & Research", "Miscellaneous", "Quick Links".
- **alexandria, shipped**: "Trailblazing", "Gaining traction", "Left
  behind", "Read these yourself".
- **alexandria, intended**: "Context beats architecture this week", with
  a hard gate that refuses the four old names before output.

What the slide says:

- **Only two newsletters here print the same headings every issue, and
  they are TLDR and us.** TLDR can afford it because it is a feed and
  sells itself as one. We sell judgment.
- Money Stuff shows the subtlety: "AI credit trading" is a plain label,
  and it is still craft, because it labels that day's topic and appears
  in no other issue. The sin is not plainness. The sin is reuse.
- Morning Brew's pun heading and its plain headline do two different
  jobs, one for the eye and one for the skimmer. We have only ever had
  the taxonomy.
- Import AI's two-part heading, claim then wry aside, is the cheapest
  warmth on this page and we have no equivalent.

### 2c. How each one lands an item

- **The Batch**: "**We're thinking:** Meta says it will release Muse
  Spark's weights eventually. But it's the harness, more than the model,
  that keeps the Muse agent safe."
- **Import AI**: "**Why this matters, maybe agent escapes are the new
  normal:** Incidents like this suggest that as we make AI systems more
  capable it becomes increasingly likely that the AI agents powered by
  them will increasingly find ways to create their own communication
  systems."
- **Morning Brew**: "What will the nepo Buffett do? CEO Abel is still
  running the company, while Howie will be in charge of protecting
  Berkshire's culture and values." Signed off with the writer's initials,
  "-BC".
- **TLDR AI**: "Claude enhanced biomolecular modeling by optimizing over
  30 models, achieving a 4x speed increase and creating a low-memory mode
  for predicting larger systems on a single NVIDIA GPU."
- **alexandria, shipped**: "*Environments as Scaffold: Enriching Feedback
  to Bootstrap Self-Evolving Agents in Long-Horizon Tasks*,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08404". The item ends on its citation.
- **alexandria, intended**: the item spine ends on "what a builder does
  differently now", then the source line.

What the slide says:

- Three of four majors end an item on a judgment. We end on a URL.
- The Batch ends on a judgment it is willing to be wrong about. That is
  the "We're thinking" beat, and it is the closest thing in the market
  to what alexandria's claim graph could support.
- **Our shipped items end where a bibliography ends.** The reader has to
  supply the consequence, which is the work they pay us to do.

### 2d. How each one closes

- **The Batch**: "...the beneficial applications continue to vastly
  outweigh the risks, and we should keep building. / Andrew"
- **Import AI**: a short piece of fiction, then "Things that inspired
  this story:", then "_Thanks for reading!_"
- **Morning Brew**: a word of the day ("Today's Word of the Day is:
  autumnal"), then "Written by Abigail Rubenstein, Brendan Cosgrove,
  Dave Lozo, Sam Klebanov, Matty Merritt, and Holly Van Leuven".
- **TLDR AI**: "Join 1,100,000 readers for one daily email." Nothing
  else.
- **alexandria, shipped**: "3431 papers ingested / 216 claims distilled /
  80 edges drawn this week".
- **alexandria, intended**: a written line that hands the day back, scale
  in plain words if scale appears, then "**You read to decide. Your
  agents load to act.**"

What the slide says:

- Four of five majors end on something that is not news. A name, a
  thank-you, a piece of fiction, a word of the day.
- **Ours ended on a row of counters, two of which no subscriber can
  decode.** It reads like a build log, and it was the last thing the
  reader saw.
- This one is already fixed. See the receipts slide.

## 3. Grades on five prose axes

**Headline: alexandria as shipped is the worst issue on this page, and
the fixed generator is still unproven.**

The five axes, defined from the canon so the grade is an instrument and
not an opinion:

- **Voice** (laws 1, 2, and the register): does a person appear in the
  sentences, or is a form being filled in.
- **Structure** (laws 9, 11, 12): does the shape serve this day's news,
  or does the news get poured into a fixed frame.
- **Compression** (the Economist reference, law 11): how much the reader
  learns per line, and whether anything pads.
- **Evidence-handling** (laws 6, 7, 8): is the strength of each claim
  visible in the sentence that makes it, and is the source one click
  away.
- **Reader-warmth** (the greeting ruling, law 10): does the issue know
  that a person is reading it.

| | Voice | Structure | Compression | Evidence | Warmth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Money Stuff** | A | A- | B | A- | A |
| **Morning Brew** | A | A | A | C | A |
| **Import AI** | A- | B+ | B- | A | B+ |
| **The Batch** | B | B- | A- | A | B |
| **TLDR AI** | D | C | A | C- | F |
| **alexandria (shipped)** | **F** | **D-** | **D** | **D** | **F** |
| **alexandria (intent)** | **C+** | **B** | **B-** | **A-** | **C** |

Defence of the harsh grades, one line each:

- **Voice F.** Ten traction bullets in 2026-W37 run the same shape, and
  six end on the same construction, "supported twice", "with two
  supports", "also with two supports". No human wrote that paragraph and
  the reader can tell.
- **Structure D-.** Four headings that would fit any issue ever
  published, an item count that matches no week's actual news, and one
  paper ("T1") wearing three hats in one section.
- **Compression D.** Five numbered procedure steps under an item whose
  finding is one sentence long. Morning Brew gets a whole succession
  story into four short paragraphs.
- **Evidence D.** The in-line grades are absent, and every link stops at
  the arXiv abstract page, which is the owner's "you didn't show me the
  paper" ruling being violated nine times in the reading list alone.
- **Warmth F.** No greeting, no close, no person, and a counter for a
  farewell.
- **Intent grades are provisional.** No issue has been generated by the
  patched prompt. C+ on voice is the most a prompt can claim before a
  model has run it.

## 4. The gap statement: five moves they make and we do not

**Headline: five prose moves, four of them cheap, and run 4 closes
three of the five in the generator tonight.**

### Move 1. The first-screen promise

- **Who does it:** Morning Brew ("In today's newsletter, we'll get into:"
  with three bullets). TLDR (three item names in the subject). The Batch
  (the issue title lists its three items: "Meta's Agent Security, The
  Navier-Stokes Controversy, Fraud on Claude"). Money Stuff (the deck
  under the title: "AI lab ratings, hourly levered ETFs, Treasury selling
  Treasury futures and egg fines").
- **What we do:** nothing. The reader learns what is in the issue by
  scrolling it.
- **Generator change (run 4, new):** the opening now names, in one line,
  the two or three things this issue covers, in plain words, before the
  interpretation begins. It is contents, not methodology, so it does not
  touch the no-meta-narration ruling.
- **Closed by run 2/3?** No. This is new tonight.

### Move 2. Written signposts inside a long item

- **Who does it:** Morning Brew turns a long item with fresh bolded
  lead-ins, "From one father to another:", "The future the Oracle
  designed", "Zoom out:". The Batch does the same with a fixed set,
  "What's new", "How it works", "Yes, but", "Behind the news".
- **What we do:** our long items are undivided blocks, and the prompt
  actively forbids labels inside an item.
- **Generator change (run 4, new):** the ban stays on fixed labels, and
  the prompt now asks for written turns inside any item longer than
  three paragraphs, phrased from that item's own material, never from a
  fixed set. The Batch's beats are the failure mode, Morning Brew's are
  the model.
- **Closed by run 2/3?** No. Run 2 wrote the spine and explicitly banned
  the labels, which was right and left the gap.

### Move 3. Arguing with the source

- **Who does it:** The Batch's "Yes, but:" beat, verbatim: "Meta says it
  evaluated Muse Spark 1.3's ability to resist prompt injections using an
  unpublished dataset, and it has not provided accuracy metrics for the
  classifiers that screen incoming data." Import AI does it in the first
  person.
- **What we do:** we grade how strong the evidence is. We never say what
  the paper declined to show.
- **Generator change (run 4, new):** the in-line evidence grade gains a
  second half. Where the payload supports it, name the thing the work did
  not measure, in the same breath as the number it did.
- **Closed by run 2/3?** Partly. Run 1 made the in-line grade mandatory,
  which is the positive half. The negative half is new tonight.

### Move 4. Continuity with our own past issues

- **Who does it:** Import AI cites itself inside an item, "(Import AI
  #471)". Money Stuff hyperlinks six of its own past issues in a single
  opening paragraph. The Batch's "Behind the news:" beat does the same
  job with its archive.
- **What we do:** nothing, and we are the one product on this page with a
  claim graph built to do it.
- **Generator change (run 4, new):** when an item updates, supports or
  overturns something a previous issue carried, the prose says so in one
  clause. **This is the move with the widest gap between what we can do
  and what we do.**
- **Closed by run 2/3?** Partly. Run 2 offered continuity as one of
  several optional shapes for the opening. Run 4 moves it down to the
  items, where the evidence actually lives.

### Move 5. Every number beside its comparison

- **Who does it:** Morning Brew, "growing the company's shares more than
  5,500,000%. By comparison, the S&P 500 gained 39,000% over the same
  period." The Batch, "Claude Sonnet 4.5 reached 45.9 percent success
  with the larger memory model versus 37.6 percent without it."
- **What we do:** the new-work section carries baselines. The traction
  section carries none at all, only "gaining three independent supports".
- **Generator change (run 4, new):** the baseline rule moves from one
  section to the whole issue. A number without its comparison is not
  finished.
- **Closed by run 2/3?** Partly. The instruction existed in one section
  of four.

## 5. Receipts: what improved tonight and across the day

**Headline: the four worst lines in 2026-W37 are now impossible to
generate, and tonight adds five more moves the majors already make.**

| What 2026-W37 printed | What the generator now requires | Run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "# alexandria digest - 2026-W37" | A title that states a finding, no date, no week id, checked before output | 1, 3 |
| "This week the most active ideas revolve around two themes." | A greeting true about this particular day, then orientation, then interpretation | 2 |
| "## Trailblazing", "## Gaining traction" | Headings written from the day's items, with eight banned strings refused at a hard gate | 3 |
| "3431 papers ingested / 216 claims distilled / 80 edges drawn this week" | A written line that hands the day back, then the standing close | 1, 2 |
| Nine reading-list entries, every link to /abs/ | A judged handful, every link rewritten to /html/ full text | 1 |
| Ten traction bullets of identical shape | Length follows the news, no section quota, depth follows significance | 3 |
| "gaining three independent supports" | Internal counts translated into plain English | 1 |

Added tonight, run 4, each traceable to a named passage above:

- The first-screen promise, from Morning Brew and Money Stuff.
- Written turns inside long items, from Morning Brew.
- The "what it did not show" clause, from The Batch's "Yes, but".
- Self-continuity at the item level, from Import AI's "(Import AI #471)".
- Baselines in every section, from The Batch and Morning Brew.

**The one number for the deck: of the twelve canon laws, 2026-W37 passed
zero. Runs 1 to 3 answered all twelve in the generator. Run 4 adds five
moves that no law required and every major already makes.**

**And the honest caveat for the deck: none of this is proven. No issue
has yet been written by the patched generator, so every grade in the
"intent" row is a claim about a prompt, not a measurement of a
newsletter. The first issue that runs through it is the real benchmark.**
