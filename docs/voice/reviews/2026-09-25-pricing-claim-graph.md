# Site copy: the claim graph on the pricing page

Owner's ruling, dispatched 2026-09-25: the pricing page must mention the
claim graph, the differentiator `docs/market/positioning.md` names in its
why-pay paragraph, "a queryable claim graph instead of a link list".

Drafted by the writer seat. Nothing here is set on the site. Every line
reaches the owner in chat first, the chair relays, and the frontend seat
applies what she approves (`docs/agents/copy-pipeline.md`, phase 1).

**One precondition is unmet and this draft ships anyway, on her
instruction.** `docs/voice/value.md` still reads "Status: awaiting her
approval", and the copy pipeline's phase 0 gates phase 1 on that
approval. This dispatch orders the draft by name, so the order governs
and the gap is reported rather than used as a reason not to work. The
draft is written against `value.md` as it stands, which is this seat's
own page and not yet law.

## The two surfaces, one candidate each

The page has four blocks of prose. These two are where the ruling lands.
The waitlist block and the billing note are untouched by it, and both
are flagged at the bottom of this file for separate reasons.

Live today, under the heading "Read for free. Build for $20.":

> The weekly digest stays free and arrives in full. The paid plan opens

### Surface 2: the Full access tier

Live today, five items. The candidate adds one, second in the list,

> The claim graph, for checking a finding before you build on it

The list's other four items stand as written. This is one new line in

## Why these lines and not the ones before them

Four rulings decided every word, and each is checked against the
candidate rather than described.

**Value, never mechanism.** No sentence says how anything is produced.
"Ask the claim graph what the research says about one thing" is the
reader's action and their result. Compare the shape she struck in round
eight: "Every day it reads the new papers, extracts the findings, and
checks them against everything it has read before." That names three
operations and hands over the blueprint.

**"It" is never the subject.** The subjects on the page are the digest,
the paid plan, every finding, the library, and you. The one "it" left is
the object of "behind", naming the digest.

**The growing, self-maintaining library is present.** Her sentence on
round eight was "no mention of a growing self mantaining corpus,
nothing. thats my point." Two sentences carry it here and neither is
decorative. "The library grows every day" is the growth. "When new work
overturns a finding, the library changes" is the maintenance, and the
line after it says what that is worth, which is that the upkeep is not
the reader's.

**Plain, short, serious.** Nine sentences in the intro and one line in
the tier, the longest thirteen words, no sentence carrying more than one
comma, no comma joining two independent
clauses, no colon-led construction, no fragment, no em dash. Nothing is
cute and nothing is defensive. The comparison positioning.md draws, a
claim graph instead of a link list, arrives as what the reader gets
rather than as an argument against a competitor, in four words at the end
of a sentence: "not a page of links."

**The stranger, and this is where a term of art was the risk.** "Claim
graph" is not a phrase a visitor can place, and the canon's outsider test
binds the site hardest of all, because a page has one screen to make
sense. A definition was the wrong fix twice over. She ruled "no more
technical. we want to sell", and a colon-led gloss is one of the three
tells she named as unserious. So the term is taught by use instead. The
sentence it first appears in shows a reader asking it something and
getting an answer, which is the whole of what they need to know before
they decide, and the mechanism stays off the page.

## Two things on this page the ruling did not cover

Both are reported and neither is drafted, because neither is what she
asked for.

**The intro says "weekly" and the product is daily.** The live line
reads "The weekly digest stays free", the Digest tier's label reads
"every Monday", and the owner adopted a daily cadence on 2026-09-19. Her
own approved line for the library page is "A new issue every day." The
candidate above says "The digest is free", with no cadence in it, so it
is correct either way, and the tier label beside it is still wrong. That
is a factual error in live copy rather than a matter of voice, and it
belongs to whoever sets the page next.

**The billing note reads as an argument.** "Many subscription businesses
count on being forgotten, and we would rather earn the renewal than
collect it." The claim is true and the register is not this one. She has
rejected defensive framing by name, and a sentence about what other
companies count on is a page defending itself. Not redrafted here, and
worth a round of its own if she wants one.
