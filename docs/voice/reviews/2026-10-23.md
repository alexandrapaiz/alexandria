# The one law the generator has never held, and the first count of how far it misses

**Writer run, 2026-10-23. Graded against `site/content/issues/2026-W39.md`,
which is the newest issue this runtime can reach, and against the generator
that wrote it.**

## The biggest weakness, stated first

Her outsider ruling is the law this product fails worst and the law nobody
has ever measured. The ruling of 2026-09-19 is that every term of art carries
its plain-words clause the FIRST time it appears, "no exceptions and no
assumed vocabulary", and she gave it with the sharpest words in the register:
"i feel like an outsider to something privy while reading. thats an issue",
and about one specimen, "i dont know what the heck yoy mean by gradients."

`prompts/digest.md` carries that ruling in seven separate places across about
a hundred lines. One of them orders the model to build the list mechanically
before judging anything. Another caps the result at five terms an issue and
says that more than five means the issue is carrying too much rather than
explaining too little.

The archived issue carries **24** terms of art it chose for itself, **22 of
them with no clause anywhere near their first appearance**, and two of the
twenty-four are the nouns in its own title.

Twenty-one grades in this directory failed this issue on this law, and all
but one failed it by quoting an example. The exception is ban list 81, which
counted by hand on 2026-10-01 against the print of 2026-09-30 and found
"twenty-four terms in that issue would have stopped a builder from outside
the research world. Three carried a clause."

So the number existed once, twenty-two days ago, on a different issue, taken
by one seat on one afternoon. Nothing ever took it again. That is this seat's
own recurring lesson in its sharpest form: a count is not an instrument, and
the register that holds a count nobody re-takes is holding a memory. The
instrument is the fix.

## Why the width was invisible, which is a fact about the gate and not the eye

The taste gate this seat shipped five days ago scores her rulings by finding
the text that enforces each one. Her outsider ruling scores green on it,
because the rule is unmistakably present in the generator seven times over.
A rule can be present, quoted, anchored and enforced by a gate while the
artifact it governs breaks it twenty-two times. That is the same shape as
that gate's own first false green, and it is now recorded as a repeat.

The prompt cannot hold this law, and the reason is in the prompt's own words.
The class the first-use pass says it exists to catch is the one that "does
not look like jargon on the page". Finding those words inside your own prose
is a judgment about what you know, made by the thing that knows it. Seven
rewritings have not moved it, which is far past the charter's threshold of
two, so the diff goes to the machine rather than to the prompt.

## The instrument: `python3 docs/voice/check_voice.py firstuse`

What it counts, and the boundary stated before the output rather than
discovered in a grade:

- **The inventory is mechanical.** Which words are terms of art, how many
  there are, and the sentence where the reader first meets each one. It reads
  the title, the masthead, every heading and every bold lead, because her
  ruling counts the term the reader MEETS and the reader meets the title
  first.
- **The adequacy is a reading.** Whether a clause actually defines its term
  is not a thing a regex knows. A gloss marker is reported as presence and
  never scored as a pass, which is the taste gate's lesson in a second place.

Four classes of term, and the fourth is the reason this is a command rather
than a list the model could follow.

| class | how it is found | why it is a class |
|---|---|---|
| `club` | a named list of ordinary English doing technical work | the class the generator says this pass exists to catch |
| `coined` | an internal capital or an all-caps run | method names, benchmark names, acronyms |
| `candidate` | three or more appearances and not ordinary English | the generator's own rule: the list is built by frequency, not by what looks like jargon |
| `name` | capitalised mid-sentence, written by the issue | law 11 requires the attribution, so it is not a word the writer chose |

Two buckets are reported and never failed, because an outsider pays for them
and the generator cannot fix them without breaking the law that put them
there. Institution names are the first. The second is a term that appears
only inside a paper's own title, which law 8 and the title rule require be
copied as it stands.

**The title gate, first and separately, because the title is a closed list
and the rest of the pass is not.** A title term is defined in the title's own
sentence or in the first sentence of the opening, and nowhere later, because
the reader met it before they had any reason to keep reading. The generator
says this in its own words and says it has already failed twice.

**The cap gate, at five.** More than about five terms needing a definition
means cut an item and run the pass again.

## Two defects in my own gate, found by building it, and one of them matters

**1. The clause window was one sentence for every term, and the generator
gives a title term two.** So the title gate passed on a fixture while the row
directly beneath it reported the same word as bare, on the same words. The
window is now the on-ramp for a term first seen on line 1 and the sentence
for every other term, which is what the generator says.

**2. The gloss test searched the whole sentence, and that cleared three real
violations.** This is the one worth keeping. Searching a sentence for ", which"
finds a clause that may be defining a different word, and in this issue it was:

> The newer work shows that an **on-policy** expert-correction pipeline, which
> rewrites only the failing turn in the weaker model's **rollout**, preserves
> its planning style and restores performance where **imitation** alone fails.

One sentence, three club words, one relative clause, and the clause defines
the pipeline. A sentence-wide search reported all three as glossed. The test
is now relative to the term: it reads the window from the term onward and asks
only about the next few characters, because a definition arrives adjacent to
the word it defines. That is also what let the loose markers in, so an
appositive with no "which" in it now counts, which the first cut had to
exclude to avoid matching every comma.

The three terms are bare. The issue never says what on-policy means, never
says what a rollout is, and uses "imitation" for a training method without
naming it as one.

## What is not fixed, and what this run did not touch

**The generator is unchanged, and that is the decision rather than an
omission.** The charter's structure watch says that when the same structural
fix fails twice through prompt changes alone, the pipeline change goes in the
ledger instead of a third prompt edit. This one has failed seven times. So
`prompts/digest.md` is byte-identical to main, which the weighing gate makes
trivially satisfied: 34,364 tokens on both sides, zero added, `pipeline/budget.py`
passing.

**The wiring, for the fourth time.** `firstuse` joins `enforcements`, `sweep`,
`taste` and `candidates` as a check that reads a register against a file and
is called by nothing. Those four are green today and stay green without anyone
ruling on anything. `firstuse` is **red** on the archived issue and will stay
red until an issue is written under it, so it belongs in the press's pre-send
pass rather than in the repository's CI, where it would redden a build for a
file nobody can edit. That scoping is in the ledger with the ask.

**The nine candidates.** `docs/voice/awaiting-her-word.md` holds nine rows
across seven surfaces, the oldest open for twenty-seven days, and three of
this file's commands are honestly red until she rules on them. This run drafted
no tenth candidate, and `check_voice.py candidates` confirms it.

**Eighteen days of silence from the press.** Nothing has printed since
2026-10-05, so this gate has no live artifact to run on and the newest issue
this seat can grade is the one it has graded about twenty times. That is not
this seat's surface and it is the fact that most limits its loop: an editor
whose press has stopped can improve the generator and cannot observe it.
