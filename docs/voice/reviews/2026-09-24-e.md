## The thing that changes how every grade below should be read

**This is the first issue written by the current generator.** The sha of
`prompts/digest.md` on `origin/main` is `0f642e2ce9f3`, and that is the sha
this row carries. Nine editorial runs of prompt work have merged, and the
press read them.

That closes the open ledger entry of this morning, "Nine runs of prompt fixes
have never reached the press", and it removes the excuse that every grade
since 2026-09-20 has been able to lean on. W39's earlier failures could be
blamed on the 2026-09-19 generator. These cannot. Every defect named below is
a defect of the generator as it stands on main tonight.

## The single biggest weakness

**The reading list is headed `## Read these yourself`.**

That is one of the four internal framework names, printed verbatim, at the
top of a section a subscriber reads. It is banned in four places at once.
The owner's ruling of 2026-09-19, repeated the same day in the word "AGAIN".
Canon law 12. Ban list entry 20. And `prompts/digest.md` itself, whose
heading slot for this exact section reads `written fresh and never the words
"Read these yourself"`.

She has flagged this twice. This is the third occurrence, and it is a
**regression**. The stale 2026-09-19 generator, writing the same slot from the
same material eleven hours earlier, produced `## The hour you should spend`.
The patched generator replaced a heading written from the day's news with the
skeleton's own label.

Here is the mechanism, and it is the finding worth more than the defect.

The prohibition supplies the string. The instruction sits inside the curly
braces of the heading slot, which is the one position in the template the
model is told to replace with its own writing, and the nearest quoted English
in that position is the forbidden phrase. The model wrote what was there.

That is not the only instance. Two more sentences of `prompts/digest.md`
shipped inside the issue.

- The prompt's example of a plain-meaning line, at line 178, is `"Teaching
  the structure in beat leaving it attached, which is the part nobody
  expected."` The issue prints `Teaching the structure beats leaving it
  attached, which is the part nobody expected.` The model corrected the
  prompt's typo and published the example.
- The prompt's rule at line 169 is named **The number that matters gets its
  own line**, and its example is `"It finishes **44.3% of tasks** with the
  scaffolding gone, up from 23.3%."` The issue prints `**The number that
  matters:** 44.3% with the harness gone, up from 23.3%.` The model published
  the example and bolted the rule's own name on the front as a label.

Three leaks in one issue, all from text quoted inside the prompt. Line 393 of
the same file already forbids exactly this: "no sentence quoted from this file
as an example survives into the issue." Stating the invariant did not hold it.

The reason it did not hold is worth naming plainly, because it generalizes
past this file. The examples in the prompt are drawn from W39's own material.
Canon law 14's worked rewrite used Harness-Zero, 44.3% and 23.3%, because W39
is the issue that produced the ruling. When the generator was then pointed at
W39 again, the example was not an illustration of the answer. It was the
answer. An exemplar drawn from the material the generator will be handed stops
being an exemplar.

Three of those four losses are the same move. The generator repaired a struck
sentence by deleting it, and the element the sentence was carrying went with
it.

The greeting is the clearest case. The owner struck `You spent last week
watching agents get faster by doing less at test time` because it assumed a
returning reader. The greeting itself is house law: taste.md, 2026-09-19, the
reader-facing warmth "including greeting the reader", and `prompts/digest.md`
gives it the first of the opening's four jobs. The generator deleted the
greeting. The issue now opens `The agent-building world has spent the week
arguing about scaffolding`, which is a fine sentence and is not a hello to
anybody. Canon law 13's own gate says of the repair that "the fix is always
the same and it is never deletion." It said that about threads. Nothing said
it about the greeting, so the greeting went.

The evidence grades are the same move under the density rule. Compression
cuts clauses, the grade is a clause, and four of them went.

**Why the links survived and the grades did not.** The link rule and the
evidence-grade rule sit next to each other in the prompt, at lines 788 and
790 of the generator that wrote this issue. Every item in this issue carries
its link. No item carries a grade. The
difference between the two rules is one sentence:

> Count the items. Count the links. They match, or the issue is not finished.

Links are counted. Grades are described. A rule survives compression only if
something counts it, and this issue is a controlled experiment proving it,
because the two rules are adjacent, the same model read both, and only the
counted one held.

## The patches

Four changes to `prompts/digest.md`, 61 lines added and 17 removed, each the
smallest one that would have prevented a failed grade above, each naming the
law it serves.

**1. The heading slots stop supplying the strings they forbid.** Serves canon
law 12, ban list 20, and the owner's ruling given twice. The forbidden phrase
leaves the curly braces where the model writes its heading, and the slot
states the rule positively instead. The four slot names move to the heading
gate at the end of the file, where the model is reading its own finished
output rather than writing into the position the words sit in. They are added
there as a tripwire **in addition to** the gate's question and never instead
of it, because ban list 36 is explicit that a check written from the last
failure is a memorial and the class question is the test.

All four slots are fixed, not the one that failed. Every one of them quoted
its own internal name inside its own braces: the traction slot said `never the
words "Gaining traction"`, the new-work slot `never the words "Trailblazing"`,
the fell-behind slot `never the words "Left behind"`. Fixing only the slot
that shipped would have left three loaded with the same charge, which is ban
list 36 committed by the seat that wrote ban list 36.

**2. The evidence grade gets the count the link already has.** Serves law 6,
and law 5 alongside it. One sentence, modeled on the line two rules above it
that worked on this same issue. This is the smallest change that would have
prevented the largest regression, and the issue itself is the evidence that
the mechanism works, because the counted rule held at five out of five while
the described rule went to zero.

**3. The greeting survives the repair that deleted it.** Serves the owner's
standing ruling that the issue greets the reader. Canon law 13's gate already
says the repair "is never deletion", and it said that about threads, so the
generator cleared the gate by removing the greeting entirely. One clause added
to that gate binds the slot as well as the sentence: a slot does not stop
being required because the sentence that filled it was wrong.

**4. The two leaked examples stop being usable as drafts.** Serves law 14 and
law 12. The plain-meaning example and the number-line example were both drawn
from W39's own material and both shipped verbatim. They are rewritten from
material no payload will contain, and the invariant at line 393 gets one
clause recording why, so the next person adding an example to this file knows
not to take it from the week they are grading.

`docs/voice/canon.md` is deliberately not changed. Its law 14 worked example
keeps the W39 wording because that passage is the record of the grade that
produced the law, and the record should not be rewritten to fix the
instruction. The instruction is what gets fixed.

**What is deliberately not patched.** Three failures above are not touched,
and each is named in the ledger instead, per charter step 4.

- The em dashes. The ASCII rule has been rewritten three times, at 51400c1,
  f9530fa and 4d50060, and six of them shipped anyway. A fourth paragraph in
  the prompt is not the fix.
- The masthead. It is in code. It cannot be reached from here and has now
  failed law 3 in three separate grades.
- The bullets. Canon law 14's formatting half is one day old and this is its
  first live test. It failed, but it has failed once, and the rule for
  escalation is twice.
