## The single biggest weakness

**The issue has one shape, and it never changes.**

Seventeen paragraphs, no list, no line standing on its own, nothing bolded
except four item leads, from the title to the close. Five of those
paragraphs run over 100 words and the longest runs 191.

That is not a prose failure. Read any single sentence in this issue and it
is good. Read the page as a shape, before a word of it, and it is a grey
column, and a reader looks at a grey column and decides how much of their
morning it is going to cost.

She is the second reader to say so and the first one was the same person
five days ago, in the word "density". The difference tonight is that she
also handed over the fix, which is formatting, and formatting is the one
tool this seat had never picked up.

## What went into the generator

Every failed grade above, translated into the smallest change that would
have prevented it.

- **The density bullet now carries tonight's ruling and its cost.**
  Correct is the floor, not the grade. It names two counts that make the
  rule checkable instead of arguable: one result per paragraph, and no
  paragraph past about 100 words. Serves law 14 and law 12a.
- **A new formatting bullet, six rules.** Prose for the argument and lists
  for the inventory. Bullets where the results are parallel, with a short
  bold lead on the same line. The number that matters on a line of its
  own, bolded inside the sentence and never as a whole bold line. A line
  of plain meaning with no number in it after every result. Numbers only
  where they change a decision. White space between sections. Serves law
  14.
- **The new-work section stops forbidding lists.** It said "Each item is
  **prose, not bullet points**" and "Nothing else becomes a list", which
  is the line that produced the wall. It now says depth is prose and
  inventory is a list, and tells the generator to judge which the day's
  material is. Serves law 14.
- **The fell-behind section's second kind is a list past two entries.**
  That section is parallel by construction, one old approach against one
  new one per entry, and it produced the 167-word paragraph. Serves law
  14 and law 8, because a list entry has somewhere obvious to put its
  link.
- **A shape gate before output, and it is hard.** Four counts with one fix
  each, then two questions the counts miss: does every result have its
  plain-meaning line, and is there one line where the number stands by
  itself. It also bans the bold-alone line outright, because the heading
  gate underneath it would let a well-written pull through. Serves law 14
  and law 12.
- **The opening is exempt.** No bullets, no pull, no bold lead. It is the
  argument and the argument is written. Without this the next issue
  arrives with a bulleted opening, which is the overcorrection this rule
  is cheap insurance against.
- **The source line now uses the separator the email template can read.**
  See below.

## The email template, which she asked this run to check

Checked by running real issue markdown through `pipeline/email_render.py`,
not by reading it. It does not render the new formatting well, in three
ways, and the full write-up with the fixes is filed for the frontend seat
in `docs/ideas.md`.

- **Bullets lose their bullets.** A top-level `- ` line becomes a separate
  item, so a parallel list renders as a stack of paragraphs with no
  markers. The bold leads survive, so the degradation is graceful rather
  than broken, and the generator's new rules were written to stay readable
  under it.
- **A bold line standing alone becomes a grey uppercase group label**, and
  then sticks to every item after it. This is why ban list 48 bans the
  shape and why the pull quote is specified as a sentence with the number
  bolded inside it.
- **The source line never reached the source slot.** The template's parser
  wants a dash between the title and the link and `prompts/digest.md` said
  comma, so every citation in every weekly issue fell into the body as
  plain text and `ITEM_SOURCE` has never once rendered. Fixed here: the
  generator now writes `*title* - [full text](url)`. The rewritten W39
  fills six source slots; the published issue fills zero.

## One structural note, not yet a structure-watch

Density has now failed through prompt changes twice, on 2026-09-19 and
again tonight. The charter says that when the same structural fix fails
twice through prompts alone, the pipeline change goes to the engineer
instead of a third round of prompt-tinkering.

This is not that case yet, and the distinction matters. The first fix told
the generator to avoid density and gave it no other shape to write in. The
second hands it the shapes and a gate that counts. That is a different
instruction, not the same one repeated louder.

If the next issue comes back dense anyway, it is the third attempt, and
the ledger entry for the engineer is a pre-send check that counts
paragraph lengths and refuses an issue with no second shape on the page.
The quality gate in PR #60 is where it would live.
