# Editorial review — 2026-09-19

First run of the writer seat (ADR-28). Graded issue: **2026-W37**,
"Richer feedback boosts long-horizon agents [September 7–13, 2026]",
written 2026-09-14 by `openai/gpt-oss-120b` against prompt
`83a0aa3be13c`, read from the digests table. Read cold and whole
before any line was judged.

The owner's dispatch asked for two issues. The daily sample v2 is
committed nowhere, so its shape is reconstructed from canon law 9 in
§2 and used as the yardstick the weekly is measured against. The
weekly is graded against all ten laws in §3.

## 1. The single biggest weakness

**The weekly is a feed of what the pipeline found, ordered by
novelty, and the owner's first principle is impact.** Law 5 says
traction leads. This issue leads with "Trailblazing" and demotes
traction to second, where it becomes ten identical bullets ending in
an unexplained "(3 supports)". Nothing in that section says what
earned any item its slot.

The lead section then spends two of its three slots on the same
paper. Items one and two both cite *T1: Terminal Agent Reinforcement
Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks*, `arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042`. The
whole of Trailblazing rests on two papers out of 3,558 ingested, and
the opening calls them "several teams".

This is the gap the owner has not yet named. Her 2026-09-19 ruling on
traction created the daily's Compounding section, so the daily was
born obeying the relevance law. The weekly never was. It kept the
section order it had before the ruling, and so the product's flagship
issue is the one issue that still reads as a release-date feed.

## 2. The daily's shape, reconstructed (canon law 9)

Law 9 fixes the daily's sections. Taking the law as written, the
daily runs in this order:

1. **Gaining traction**. Work that is gaining acceptance, ordered by
   impact, each slot stating what earned it.
2. **Trailblazing**. The genuinely new, labeled as unproven, so
   freshness is never disguised as importance (taste.md, 2026-09-19).
3. **Left behind**. What lost ground.
4. **Read these yourself**. A chosen reading list, not a dump.
5. **The dual-audience close**. "You read to decide. Your agents load
   to act." (law 10).

*Corrected in run 2.* This list first read "Compounding", "New and
unproven", and a standing "instrument note" section. The owner rejected
all three the same day: the first two as headings that read like a
machine sorting rows, the third as the issue narrating its own method.
Her four section names above are the law, and they were hers before
this seat existed. The reconstruction was wrong to invent names for
sections she had already named, which is the exact mistake the
corrected law 9 now forbids.

Two things follow, and both are findings about the weekly rather than
the daily. First, the daily's order is the relevance law made
structural: traction is section one, novelty is section two, and the
label on section two does the honesty work. The weekly inverts that
order and carries no label. Second, the daily ends on the reader and
the agent. The weekly ends on pipeline counts.

The weekly is not the daily and must not become it. Canon says the
daily may list and the weekly must argue. But "argues" is a stronger
requirement than "lists", not a lighter one, and the weekly currently
does neither.

## 3. The weekly graded, law by law

### Law 1 — Plain sentences, transition words, no stylistic em dashes, no semicolon joins. **FAIL**

The em dash is used as a parenthetical bracket in the opening: "a
**dense verification reward**—counting how many assertions an answer
gets right—produces a **28.5 % relative gain**". Unspaced, on both
sides, exactly the stylistic use the law names. Every source line
carries another: "*paper*: **T1...** — [link]". Both en-dash
lead-ins do the same job: "**Contradicted** – First, the claims newer
evidence says are wrong:".

Counted across the issue: five em dashes, twelve en dashes.

The cause is worth naming, because it is the most useful thing this run
found. **The prompt banned the em dash in roughly thirty of its own
sentences.** "Avoid stylistic em dashes" sat in a bullet that read
"connect them with transition words (so, because, instead, as a
result, however) — but don't reach for the same transition word
twice". A model takes its register from the prose it is shown at least
as much as from the rule it is told, and every instruction block in
that prompt demonstrated the habit the rule forbids. The patch in this
PR rewrites the prompt's own prose to obey the law, leaving the dash
only where it labels a payload field and in one example deliberately
marked as wrong. That is a bigger diff than a rule change and it is
the only version of the fix that can work.

Underneath that sits a typography failure nobody has flagged. The
body contains **87 non-breaking hyphens** (U+2011) in place of
ordinary hyphens, in "Long‑horizon", "reinforcement‑learning",
"sparse‑reward", and every other compound, plus **19 narrow
no-break spaces** (U+202F) producing "28.5 %", "64 %", "122 B".
These are not cosmetic. A reader searching for "long-horizon" in the
archive does not match this text, and the second audience in law 10
loads these bytes. The sentence rules never told the model to write
in plain ASCII punctuation, so it reached for the typesetter's
characters.

### Law 2 — No buzzwords, every line targeted and actionable. **FAIL**

"Read these yourself" is where the targeting dies. Nine entries, and
the lines that justify them say nothing a builder can act on:
"advancing agent design and evaluation pipelines", "exposing design
patterns useful for infrastructure", "a technique that could reshape
RL pipelines". The last one also stacks a hedge onto hype.

The section name "Trailblazing" is itself a claim of excitement the
items have to earn afterward, which is ban-list entry 8.

### Law 3 — Sell the product, never the recipe. **FAIL**

The issue's second line is the recipe: "*The latest in AI research,
read in full and distilled weekly: what's new, what's gaining
acceptance, and what newer evidence has overturned.*" It describes
how the digest is made and then recites its own table of contents.
The issue then closes on the recipe too: "3558 papers ingested • 310
claims distilled • 102 edges drawn".

Both are outside this seat's reach. The masthead is `MASTHEAD` at
`pipeline/weekly.py:311` and the footer is required by the prompt's
own structure block. The masthead is filed for the engineer in
docs/ideas.md, and the prompt change below handles the footer half.

### Law 4 — The headline is a finding, not a greeting. **FAIL**

"Richer feedback boosts long-horizon agents" names a direction, not a
finding. A builder cannot decide from it whether to open the issue,
which is the whole test. The material for a real headline was sitting
in the first item: a dense reward lifting task resolution from 49.4%
to 64.0%.

This costs more than a line. `pipeline/weekly.py:377` uses the H1 as
the **email subject**. The headline is the only thing most readers
ever see, and the prompt's instruction to "lead with the sharpest
concrete detail this week actually has" was advisory enough to lose
to a category label.

### Law 5 — Relevance is impact, traction leads, every ranking says what earned each slot. **FAIL**

The central failure, stated in §1. Three specifics.

Traction does not lead: Trailblazing is section one.

No slot explains itself. Every traction item is the same shape, a
compressed claim plus a parenthetical count: "Puffin-World's native
3D world states remove the need for external offline modules. (3
supports)". Ten of these, the first four all reading "(3 supports)".
The ranking is invisible because the ordering key is invisible.

"(3 supports)" is internal vocabulary. It is the count of incoming
`supports` edges in the claim graph, which is a fact about
alexandria's database, not about the research. No reader can decode
it, and the prompt asked for the opposite: "say what the accumulating
agreement means in one plain sentence."

### Law 6 — Evidence grades in-line. **FAIL, with nothing to grade against**

Not one item in the issue carries an evidence grade. The strongest
number in the issue, the 28.5% relative gain, is stated with no note
that it is the authors' own experiment on their own agent, unreplicated,
reported in the same paper that proposes the method.

The missing grade would have caught a real problem. T1 "resolves 64 %
of tasks on Terminal-Bench 2.1, surpassing GPT-3.5-Turbo and
approaching Claude Opus". A 2026 agent measured against GPT-3.5-Turbo
is measured against a baseline chosen to flatter it. An evidence
grade is the mechanism that makes a weak baseline visible, and the
issue has no such mechanism.

### Law 7 — Honesty notes are product. **FAIL**

Three notes the issue owed the reader and did not write.

Two papers carry the entire Trailblazing section while the footer
advertises 3,558 papers ingested. The opening says "several teams"
when it means two.

"No citation movers were recorded this week." That is stated and
abandoned. A reader cannot tell whether the field was quiet or the
citation instrument did not run, and law 7 exists for exactly that
sentence.

The third Contradicted bullet is not a contradiction. "The same
reference implementation that achieved 82.2 % was later shown to drop
to 12.5 % on memory-intensive tasks" reports a scope limit on a
result the bullet above it already cited. The pipeline drew a
`contradicts` edge and the writer passed it through without judging
it, which is how the same implementation appears twice in one section,
once as the overturner and once as the overturned.

### Law 8 — Links go to the full text. **FAIL, the sharpest one**

Three links in the issue, all of them abstract pages:
`arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042` twice and `arxiv.org/abs/2609.08404`.
Zero `arxiv.org/html/` links.

Then the worse half. **"Read these yourself" contains nine papers and
not one link.** The section whose only job is to send the reader to
the paper gives the reader no way to reach a single one. The prompt's
rule "Every item cites its paper with a markdown link" sits in the
Rules block, while the section's own instruction says "title, link,
one plain line", and the model followed neither. The owner's ruling
was "You didn't show me the paper." Nine times over, it did not.

### Law 9 — Structure, and the weekly must argue. **FAIL**

The weekly lists. Gaining traction is ten bullets, Left behind is six
bullets, Read these yourself is nine bullets. Twenty-five of the
issue's items are bullets and three are prose. Canon takes structure
from Stratechery here: an argument built across sections. Nothing
carries from Trailblazing into Gaining traction, and nothing carries
from either into Left behind. The three sections could be reordered or
published separately with no loss, which is the test for whether an
argument exists.

Measured against the daily's reconstructed shape in §2, the weekly is
missing the honesty label on its new work, the instrument note, and
the close, and it has the first two sections in the wrong order.

### Law 10 — "You read to decide. Your agents load to act." **FAIL**

Absent. The string does not appear. The issue's last line is the
pipeline's own counts, so the one issue this seat can read ends by
talking about itself.

### Ban list

- **Entry 3, symmetric paragraph rhythm.** The opening is four
  paragraphs of near-identical length, three of them opening the same
  way: "A team led by Junyao Yang demonstrated...", "A team led by
  Hongbang Yuan introduced...", "Finally, researchers at the same
  group released...". The prompt warns against this pattern twice and
  the model produced it anyway.
- **Entry 7, summaries that restate the headline.** "Feedback-Enriched
  Environments let agents explore proactively" is followed by "agents
  learn to wander into broader state regions".
- **Entry 4, the hedge stack.** "a technique that could reshape RL
  pipelines", "may replace flat memory-based planning".
- **Entry 12, uniform enthusiasm.** Ten traction bullets at identical
  length and identical shape.
- **Attribution rule broken.** The prompt says institutions first and
  author names only "when institutions are missing". The opening uses
  author names twice.

Four new tells are appended to the ban list this run: typesetter
punctuation, internal pipeline vocabulary at the reader, the complete
dump instead of a judged selection, and one paper wearing several
hats.

## 4. What changed in the generator

Every grade above that failed is answered by a change to
prompts/digest.md in this PR. The mapping:

| Law | Change |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Traction section moved to first and renamed **Compounding**; each item must state what earned its slot; `(N supports)` must be rendered as plain English; new work renamed **New and unproven** and labeled |
| 8 | `arxiv.org/abs/ID` must be rewritten to `arxiv.org/html/ID` with abs as fallback; every "Read these yourself" entry must carry a link, no exceptions |
| 4 | The title must carry the week's sharpest concrete result, with the decide-from-one-line test and a note that it ships as the email subject |
| 6 | An in-line evidence grade is now required per item, with a short vocabulary, and weak or obsolete baselines must be named |
| 7 | A standing **Instrument note** section, with three named triggers: thin sourcing, a silent stream, and a relation the writer judged wrong |
| 10 | The dual-audience close is now the mandatory last line, after the footer |
| 1 | ASCII punctuation required, the em-dash ban extended to parenthetical pairs and source lines |
| 2, 9 | Read these yourself capped at five with a "why you" line; the weekly must carry one argument named in the opening and picked up by every section |
| 1, all | The prompt's own prose rewritten to obey the punctuation law, because it was teaching the violation by example |
| ban 16 | One paper may not hold two slots without the prose saying both come from the same work |
| prompt | Institution-first attribution moved from advice inside a section to a standing rule |

The section order changed, so I checked that nothing reads it. No
Python, JavaScript, or template in the repo matches on "Trailblazing",
"Gaining traction", "Left behind", or "Read these yourself".
`send_newsletter` touches only the H1 and list spacing, so the rename
is safe.

Two of these also serve the daily the engineer is building, and the
prompt now marks them as cadence-independent: the section order in law
9 and the close in law 10 are written as house rules rather than
weekly-only structure.

## 6. Grade

Zero of ten laws passed.

That number is worth stating plainly rather than softening. The issue
is not badly written line by line. Its sentences are clear, its
numbers are real, and its procedures are genuinely useful. It fails
because the laws describe a product with judgment, honesty, and a
route to the evidence, and the generator was asked for a summary of a
database. The prompt that produced it was written before the ten laws
existed, so this run is the first time the generator has been asked to
answer to them.

## 7. Run 2: the prose guide applied, and one thing that is missing

Second run the same day, on the owner's order: merge main, apply the
market seat's newsletter prose guide to the generator on top of her
fine-tuning, verify run 1's patches survived, and read the whole
prompt end to end.

### The guide is a stub

`docs/market/newsletter-prose-guide.md` is on main as ordered, but it
carries a header, a note on why it was commissioned, and the sentence
"Work in progress — populating from primary sources now." There are no
guidance points in it. PR #38's own description says "Research in
progress — marking ready when the guide is complete", and it was
merged before that happened.

So the craft work in this run does not come from the guide. It comes
from the two sources that were available: the canon's References
section, which already names what to take from Morning Brew, Money
Stuff, The Economist, Stratechery and Import AI, and the owner's
rulings in taste.md, which name the four craft problems directly.
That is stated here rather than implied, because when the guide is
populated it should be applied on its own terms, and whoever does that
needs to know this run did not read it.

### What the four craft areas became

**Opening and greeting.** The opening had two jobs and now has three:
greet, orient, interpret. The greeting is one short line, addressed to
the reader, and it has to be true about this particular day. The rule
names its own failure case, because "Welcome to another edition" is
what a model reaches for when told to be warm. Her context-first
invariant is untouched and now governs all three jobs.

**Heading voice.** Her four section names are stated as fixed law with
"Compounding" and "New and unproven" named in the prompt as rejected,
so the next model to read it cannot reinvent them. Heading craft moved
to where it can still be exercised: the item headlines, which state a
finding in plain words and carry no explaining colon.

**Item anatomy.** One spine, at whatever length an item earns: context,
what changed with its institution, the evidence grade in the same
breath, the builder's consequence, the source line. The order never
inverts. It is written as a spine and not a template, because a
template is what the voice section exists to prevent.

**Sign-off.** The issue ended on "papers ingested / claims distilled /
edges drawn". Two of those three are alexandria's internal vocabulary,
which ban-list 14 already forbids printing at the reader, and a row of
counts is a cold way to leave someone. It now ends on a written line
that hands the day back, scale in plain words if scale appears at all,
then the standing close unreworded.

### Run 1's patches, verified

All five survive the merge and still comply. Gaining traction is the
first section in the template. The full-text link rule binds every
section. The title is a finding that ships as the subject line.
Evidence grades are in-line and mandatory on any item with a number.
No meta narration, and that rule is now stronger than it was, for the
reason below.

### What the end-to-end read caught

Six seams, and one of them mattered more than the rest. The
no-meta-section rule was sitting as bare bold text inside the fenced
template, where every other instruction is wrapped in braces. A model
following "exactly this structure" could have printed the paragraph
banning meta-commentary as a section of the newsletter. It is in Rules
now.

The second was the same failure one level down. Left behind opened by
telling the reader why the section was worth reading. Her ruling calls
section intros that justify the section the same disease as ranking
narration, so the prompt was contradicting itself across two adjacent
paragraphs. Both of these were only visible by reading the file as one
document, which is the argument for the step.

### Ban list

Three tells appended: the greeting that carries no information, the
section that defends itself, and headings that name a category instead
of speaking.

### Grade for run 2

Not a graded issue. No new issue has been published since 2026-W37, so
there is nothing to read cold that run 1 did not already read. The
next graded run should be the first issue written by the patched
prompt, and the thing to check first is whether the greeting sounds
like a person or like a model told to sound like a person.
