## 0. The biggest weakness, stated first

**An issue makes one claim about alexandria itself, and it was false. The
grading instrument has five passes and all five read the prose.**

Every other sentence in an issue is about somebody's research, carries a
link, and can be checked by a reader who cares enough to click. The scale
line in the close is the one sentence with no link behind it. A reader
either trusts it or stops trusting the issue, so it is the single
sentence in the whole product where being wrong costs the most, and it is
the only one nothing was checking.

The count and the verb, side by side:

| | the act | the count |
|---|---|---|
| ingested | a title and an abstract arrived | 8,956 papers held, 4,227 in the last seven days |
| triaged | sorted after arrival | 3,983 |
| read in full | the full text actually read | **166**, of which 164 produced claims |
| claims | findings taken out of what was read | 746 |
| links | connections drawn between findings | 238 |

The printed sentence took a number from the first row and a verb from the
third. Nothing was misread and no number was invented. One count was
handed the neighbouring count's verb, and the sentence reads so naturally
that six passes of prose checking had no way to see it.

The masthead is the same defect, older and wider. "The latest in AI
research, read in full and distilled weekly" has printed above every
issue ever sent. It is true of 166 papers out of 8,956.

## 4. The claims pass, third question

The pass added on 2026-09-24 takes every comparison the issue makes and
asks whether the two things measure the same quantity. It was pointed at
the research. Tonight it gets a third question, pointed at alexandria,
and the first thing it catches is the sentence that prompted this
dispatch.

The counts above were taken from the corpus rather than from the payload,
because the payload's numbers are what got printed and the question is
whether they mean what the sentence said. The queries are in section 7.
Two of them are worth stating here in words.

**166 papers carry a distill timestamp and 164 of them produced claims.**
The gap of two is papers that were read and yielded nothing, which is an
honest outcome and not an error.

**The gap between ingestion and full reading is not a backlog, it is the
design.** 4,973 papers are sitting in the triage queue and 8 in the
distill queue. Triage exists so that most papers are never read in full,
on purpose, because reading everything is neither possible nor useful.
That matters for the repair. The gap will never close, so a sentence that
depends on the reader assuming it has closed will never become true, and
qualifying it is not a fix. The canon already says the fix for a claim
that needs a qualifier to survive is to drop the claim. Law 15 says it
about this one.

**The close is promoted to a graphic in the email.** In
`pipeline/email_render.py` the first line after the final rule is
extracted as the `STATS` region and set in its own block by the
template. So the one sentence in the issue with no link behind it is also
the one the email design emphasises most. That is not an argument for
removing the line. It is the reason it has to be exactly right.

## 5. Where the false sentence came from

It was not a hallucination. The generator instructed it, in
`prompts/digest.md`, in the closing slot, verbatim until tonight:

> "3,558 papers read to get to these five" is a fact about the product
> and earns its place.

A model reading that file at the position where the close gets written
found a finished sentence with a blank where a number goes, and the
payload had a number that fitted. It substituted and shipped. The
instruction two lines below it, "it says how much was read, never how the
reading was done", confirmed that "read" was the right verb.

This is ban list 56 for the third time: an example sitting at a writing
position is a template whatever the instruction beside it says. The first
two occurrences were recorded this morning, in run 13's incident entry,
and both were fixed in the slot where they were found. This one was in
the same file, four hundred lines further down, and nothing in either fix
looked for it. The incident is filed as
`INC-2026-09-26-fix-found-in-one-slot`, and its point is not that a third
example existed. It is that a file was patched twice in one day for a
defect that is a property of the file.

The second half of the cause is the payload description. The prompt
called `stats` "this week's pipeline counts" and named no count. A writer
handed five numbers under one label will treat them as five ways of
saying the same size.

**3. A reading gate at the end of the file.** Law 15, and the owner's

## 8. The numbers, and where they came from

Read from the corpus at 2026-09-26, so the review can be checked rather
than believed.

```sql
select count(*) from papers;                              -- 8956
select count(*) from papers
  where fetched_at > now() - interval '7 days';            -- 4227
select count(*) from triage_log;                           -- 3983
select count(*) from papers where distilled_at is not null;-- 166
select count(distinct paper_id) from claims;               -- 164
select count(*) from claims;                               -- 746
select count(*) from claim_links;                          -- 238
select count(*) from triage_queue;                         -- 4973
select count(*) from distill_queue;                        -- 8
```

The ingestion count in the printed payload is the first two: `gather()`
in `pipeline/weekly.py` selects `count(*) from papers where fetched_at >
now() - interval '7 days'` and labels it `papers_ingested`. That query is
the proof of what the number means. It counts rows in `papers`, and a row
in `papers` is a title and an abstract.
