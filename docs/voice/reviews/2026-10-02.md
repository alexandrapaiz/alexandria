## The single biggest weakness

**Four of the issue's paragraphs open on a bolded label, and the gate written
to catch labels cannot see any of them, because it collects lines and
colon-fragments and these are bold runs punctuated as sentences.**

The four, in order, each one the first thing the eye lands on in its section:

> **The comparison that matters.**
> **What this means for builders.**
> **The design choice.**
> **The abstraction choice matters.**

Put the heading gate's own deciding question to each: could this exact line
sit over a different day's items without changing a word? All four, yes.
"**What this means for builders.**" would have fitted every issue this product
will ever send. The gate says a label is a label "at any level and in any
typeface", and that is the correct rule. It is the collection step in front of
the rule that fails, and it fails on punctuation.

The step has two halves. The first collects "every line beginning with `#`,
and every run of bold or italic text sitting alone on its own line". None of
the four sits alone, because each is followed on the same line by the
paragraph's first sentence. The second half was added for exactly that case
and it collects by colon: "For every line in the issue that contains a colon,
take the text in front of that colon as a thing to check on its own." None of
the four contains a colon. Each ends in a full stop.

So the specimen the file names by hand, "**The number that matters:**", and
the one that printed on 2026-09-30, "**The comparison that matters.**", are
the same label with one character changed, and that character is the whole
difference between collected and invisible.

**The count, and it is the part that makes this a regression rather than a
standing weakness.** The published page carries two bold leads and both of
them are findings written from the day's news: "**Regularized search finds
better harnesses without gaming the benchmark.**" and "**Multi-stage training
forgets, and the last stage does not fix it.**" Neither would fit another day.
They pass the gate's question. The newest print, written by the generator two
commits later, carries four and none of them is a finding.

```
paragraph-opening bold runs, excluding the standing close
  site/content/issues/2026-W39.md   2   both findings
  press_rehearsals id 3             4   four labels
bold runs containing a colon
  both artifacts                    0
```

**Why this is the seventh disguise and not a new defect.** The gate's own text
says the failure "has now arrived six times in six disguises, and each one
walked past the check written for the one before", and it lists them: a
section heading, a taxonomy word, a bold word over a group, an italic word
over a numbered list, a bolded label welded to a sentence with a colon, and a
plain one with a colon and no bold. The seventh is the bolded label welded to
a sentence with a full stop. The file predicted this in the sentence after the
list, that "the next one will wear a disguise that is not on any list, so a
line that passes only because it failed to match a string has not been checked
at all", and then the collection step in front of it went on matching strings.

**And the shape has a cause worth naming, because it is the open escalation.**
Law 14 licenses the bold lead in one place only: "Parallel results go in a
bulleted list, each bullet opening with a short bold lead." This issue sets
zero lists, for the sixth consecutive grade. The ornament the law attached to
the list arrived without the list, four times, at the top of four paragraphs.
That is one more row of evidence for the filing run 20 escalated to the
engineer, and this run ships no seventh wording of the formatting gate.

The patch does not add an eighth string. It replaces both halves of the
collection step with the structural unit they were both reaching for: any run
of bold or italic that BEGINS a line, whatever punctuates it and whether or
not the line continues.

## The second finding: the one slot where house law has nowhere to go

**The reading list prints three picks. None of them carries an evidence grade,
while all five sections above carry one, and the reason is that this slot's
format spec has no place to put it.**

The top of `prompts/digest.md` lists what binds every issue at every cadence:
"The four sections below in their given order, the greeting, the in-line
evidence grades, the link rule, and the closing line are house law." Canon law
6 is the same rule. The format the slot then specifies is one line:

> Format: *title* - [full text](url), then one line naming the decision this
> paper would inform.

A title, a link, and the reader's decision. There is no slot for what the work
cannot establish, so a model obeying the format cannot obey the law, and three
entries out of three printed with no grade.

```
named pieces of work in the issue                    9
carrying an arxiv.org/html link                      8
carrying a grade of any kind                         4
  in the five sections                               4 of 6
  in the three reading-list picks                    0 of 3
```

This matters more here than anywhere else in the issue, because this is the
section that asks the reader for an hour, and each of the three entries spends
its second sentence on a general claim about the field with no number behind
it and nothing to qualify it:

> The planning bottleneck is not reasoning speed. It is repeated execution of
> plans that fail for structural reasons this system catches first.

One paper, unreplicated, and the sentence is about planning. That is ban list
83 one level up, the general sentence over the particular, with the particular
left out altogether.

**Three more failures in the same six lines, and each one is a rule walked
around rather than broken.**

*The title slot took a codename.* Five body items print the paper's full title
in italic. The three picks print a bolded short name: "**Hard Stop**",
"**GAVEL**", "**Just-In-Time Agent Memory**". The format says `*title*` and
these are bold. "GAVEL" is an unexpanded acronym standing at the position
where the reader decides whether to spend the hour, which is the one thing the
regurgitation rule forbids everywhere else in the file: "Ideas lead, and
coined terms, acronyms, and system names follow only after the idea has been
said plainly."

*The banned frame moved up into the heading.* The slot bans one sentence by
name, "Worth the hour if you are...", and bans its find-and-replace variants
too. The entries obeyed. The heading printed "## Three papers worth the hour".
The row of 2026-09-28 is the before picture, with the heading reading "What to
read if you have one hour" and the banned line on two entries out of two. The
phrase did not leave the slot. It moved to the one line in the slot the ban
does not name. Run 21 graded this heading as a slot name printed as a heading
and it is that too, and the migration is why it was available.

*The verb list was obeyed by dropping the verb.* The slot bans verbs of
presentation and lists them: "Shows", "details", "presents", "provides",
"introduces" and every synonym. Not one appears. All three entries open on a
bare noun phrase describing the paper instead:

> The forensic autopsy of a real agent sandbox breach: ...
> A graph world model that verifies and repairs plans before execution, ...
> The case for just-in-time versus ahead-of-time memory construction, ...

Three catalogue entries with no catalogue verb in them. The ban was written on
a word class and the posture does not need a word from that class, so the test
moves to the grammatical subject: the line is about the reader, their decision
or what they are building, and never about the paper or its contents. Two of
the three then take the same shape in their second sentence as well, "If you
run agents with tool access, this is..." and "If you are building memory
systems, the ... is...", against the slot's own rule that no two entries take
the same grammatical shape.

## What this run changed, and what it deliberately did not

**Changed, two patches, both in one slot each.**

1. The heading gate's collection step. Both string-shaped halves are replaced
   by one structural unit: any run of bold or italic that begins a line,
   whatever punctuates it and whether or not the line continues. This is
   smaller than what it replaces and it subsumes the colon half rather than
   sitting beside it. Serves law 12 and the owner's rulings of 2026-09-19.

   own title in place of a bolded codename, and the subject test in place of
   the verb list, and the slot's frame ban is stated to bind the heading as
   well as the entries. Serves laws 6, 9 and 12a.

**Not changed, and each for a reason.**

- **No seventh wording of the formatting gate.** Sixth grade with the same
  three zeroes. Run 20 escalated it to the engineer after the fifth. This
  grade adds the bold-label row to that filing and nothing else.
- **No eighth string in the label tripwire.** Seven disguises have now walked
  past seven string tests. Adding the eighth string is the move this gate's
  own text says does not work.
- **No change to the grading procedure in the canon.** The coverage rule is
  already written correctly and already fixes the unit. It was applied to one
  law and not another in the same pass, so the failure is in the running and
  not in the text, and the incident is the right place for it. The procedure

- **No second patch on anything runs 20 or 21 touched.** Their patches have
