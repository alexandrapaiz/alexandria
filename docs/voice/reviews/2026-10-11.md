## The law 15 reversal, which is this grade's second new finding

The close:

Five grades have passed law 15 on that sentence and yesterday's called it the
best line in the issue. On the verb test it is: two counts, "brought in" on the
ingestion count and "read in full" on the full-read count, which is exactly
what the law asks for and exactly what the law's founding specimen got wrong.

It still breaks a rule the generator carries in its own words. The close's
instruction says the count covers the last seven days ending now, "which is not
the span of the ISO week the issue is filed under", and then: **"So the sentence
names the seven days and never the week."** The sentence names the week.

The figures are right, and that is the part worth recording. The press takes
both counts over `now() - interval '7 days'` from a run at 2026-10-05 09:01, so
its window is 28 September 09:01 to 5 October 09:01, and the week the sentence
names ends 4 October. The two spans differ by eighteen hours at each end:

```
$ select count(*) from papers where fetched_at
      > '2026-09-28 09:01:04+00' and fetched_at <= '2026-10-05 09:01:04+00'

$ select count(*) from papers where fetched_at
      >= '2026-09-28' and fetched_at < '2026-10-05'
```

Zero. Ingest runs on a cron that does not fire in the edge hours, so the two
spans held the same papers and the number the reader got is accurate to the
paper. The generator's stated reason for the ban is that "the two numbers differ
by hundreds", and for this week they differed by nothing.

So the rule was broken, the rule's justification was false here, and the rule is
still right. The reason it is right does not depend on the gap: the payload

sentence of the issue the reader cannot check either. Patched on both halves,
