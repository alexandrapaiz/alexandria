## The single biggest weakness

**The issue is a Monday weekly, the one demand the generator makes of a
Monday weekly is that it argue one case instead of listing findings, and it
lists. The gate cannot see that it is Monday, because nothing in the payload
says so.**

`prompts/digest.md` opens by dividing itself. Everything in it is house law
except one thing, stated in its own words at line 16:

> Weekly-only is the demand that the issue argue one case rather than list
> findings. A daily may list. Monday may not. Where this file says "this
> week", a daily issue reads it as "today".

The artifact is the Monday weekly. `press_rehearsals` id 3 carries
`week = 2026-W39`, `rehearse` takes its label from `week_just_ended`, and the
only cron in `pipeline/weekly.py` that writes an issue is
`modal.Cron("0 9 * * 1")`. So the clause that binds is "Monday may not."

What printed is five findings under five headings, announced by a contents
paragraph, with a thesis asserted after the fact in the closing lines:

> The field is converging on a question: how much of the harness can
> disappear into the model, and what breaks when it does?

Graded as a coverage law, with the two integers the procedure requires.
Sections in the issue: five. Sections that question reaches: two, the
distillation one and the forgetting one. The close names a third by appending
it with "And". The memory section and the ceiling section appear in no frame
the issue builds, not the opening and not the close, and they are in the
issue because they were in the payload. An argument whose conclusion does not
need three of its five sections is a list with a paragraph on top.

**Why the gate could not fire, and this is the part worth fixing.** The
payload hands over `week`, `dates`, `stats`, `new_claims`, `superseded`,
`traction`, `deprecated` and `deep_reads`. No key says which cadence is being
written. The prompt conditions its single most consequential structural
demand on a fact the model is never given, so the model guessed, and the
print shows it guessing both ways at once: "this week" three times and
"Today:" once, in one issue, which is a daily's furniture inside a weekly.

This is the company standard on gates, that a gate is judged by what it can
see and the test is to name a change that would break what the gate governs
and ask whether the gate would have seen it. A weekly that lists is exactly
that change. The gate cannot see the calendar, so it cannot see the change.
It has reported a quiet pass ever since it was written.

**Run 20 graded this law PASS.** Its verdict reads "Law 9, the fine-tuned
instruction is the foundation. PASS. Context-first holds", and context-first
does hold. Law 9 has two halves, the house-law half about the owner's
fine-tuning and the weekly-only half about arguing, and the verdict graded
the half that produces a quotation. That is the third run of the family the
register already holds twice, where a grade clears a law by inspecting what
exists instead of counting what is missing. It is recorded as an incident in
this PR.

the other bare in all eight of its appearances.** The title is:

"Harness" is handed over on first use, in a clause an outsider can use: "a
heavy harness at deployment: the scaffolding that retries, checks, and steers
the model through long tasks." That is the rule obeyed, and run 20 was right
to say so.

"Distillation" is never defined anywhere in 1,219 words. It appears in the
title, in the contents line, in the first section heading, three times in
that section's body, in a bold lead, and in the close. The nearest thing to a
clause is "a distillation method that moves a base model from 23.3% to 44.3%
task success", which says what the method achieves and not what the word
means. A stranger meets this word as the second word of the issue and leaves
without it.
