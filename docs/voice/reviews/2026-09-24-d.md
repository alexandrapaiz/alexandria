## The single biggest weakness

**The issue's second section opens on a comparison that is not a
comparison, and says so itself before asserting it anyway.**

Quoted whole, because the failure is the move and not any one line:

> Start with the number that turned out to be wrong. A prior benchmark
> result held that an expert-authored reference implementation achieves
> 82.2% success on τ^τ-Bench, establishing a high ceiling for agent
> construction tasks. DRG-MAPPO, a hierarchical multi-agent
> reinforcement learning method for cooperative air combat, now reports
> 87% win rate in high-fidelity simulations. The contexts differ, agent
> construction versus combat simulation, but the broader belief that
> expert-authored baselines set immovable ceilings took a hit this week.

82.2% is a task success rate on a benchmark for building agents. 87% is a
win rate for simulated fighter aircraft flying against a simulated
adversary. The two numbers share a percent sign and nothing else. There
is no measure on which 87 is larger than 82.2, because they are not
measurements of the same thing, and no belief about agent-construction
ceilings can be touched by an air-combat result.

The issue knows this. "The contexts differ" is the writer noticing, and
what follows the comma is the writer proceeding. That is the shape worth
naming, because it is not carelessness. A hedge was written, the hedge
was true, and the hedge was then treated as having earned the claim it
qualifies. **A sentence that needs that hedge does not have the finding.**

This matters more than any density count, and it is the reason it leads.
The fell-behind section is the part of the product no competitor has.
Every other newsletter can tell a reader what is new. This one claims to
tell a reader what stopped being true, and a reader trusts that section
or the product has no second reason to exist. It led this week with a
number that did not fall.

## Why four passes missed it

They were all prose passes.

The canon's grading procedure runs four, in order: the outsider read, the
taste gate, the laws, the ban list. Every one asks how the issue is
written. Not one asks whether what it says is so. The instrument is
complete on craft and has no reading at all for a claim that is well
built and wrong, so a false comparison in clean prose is invisible to it
by construction.

The rewrite proves the point rather than escaping it. Run three rebuilt
this passage under law 14, split it, gave it air, and kept the claim:

> The domains are different and the comparison is loose. What broke is
> the belief that an expert-written baseline is a wall, and that belief
> was doing real work in how teams set their targets.

Better prose, same defect, and the hedge got shorter while the assertion
got firmer. A rewrite that improves the sentences carrying a wrong claim
has made the claim more persuasive, which is the worst available outcome.

This is the third time in two days that a check passed something it had
no way to fail. Incident 26, the ASCII gate narrower than the ASCII rule.
Incident 32, the quality checker whose parser read zero items and
reported zero findings. Now the grading canon itself. Recorded as
`INC-2026-09-24-grading-has-no-truth-pass`.

## What went into the generator

One patch, and deliberately only one.

`prompts/digest.md` already tells the writer to judge every edge before
printing it, and the case it covers is the `contradicts` edge that is
really a scope limit. That rule is about strength. The failure here is
about kind, and the file had nothing on it. A new test sits with the
existing edge rule: before an edge is printed, the two claims must be
measurements of the same thing, and where they are not, the edge is not
printed at whatever strength. The hedge shape is banned in the same
place, because the hedge is how this one got through.

I did not patch the em dashes, the density rules, or the formatting
rules. All three were patched today, twice each in some cases, and
charter step 4 says a fix that has failed twice through the prompt goes
to the ledger rather than into a third paragraph. They are in the ledger.
