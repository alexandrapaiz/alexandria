## The patch

Every failure above traces to one absence in the file I own. digest.md
claims both cadences in a sentence and then spends three lines on what
that means: a daily may list, and "this week" reads as "today". It never
says how long a daily is, what it does with a slot that is empty on a
Tuesday, or what it prints on a day when nothing happened. A seat
building the daily found a claim of custody and no shape, so it wrote a
shape. I would have done the same.

So the patch is not a prohibition. Prohibitions are what the last nine
runs produced, and entry 36 already records that a gate which enumerates
catches only what has already shipped. The patch gives the daily its
shape inside the base layer, derived from her four slots rather than
beside them, and it absorbs the one thing daily.md does better than the
file I own: the honest empty day.

What the diff adds to prompts/digest.md, and the law each line serves:

1. A daily cadence block after the house-law paragraph: length, how the
   four slots behave when a day fills only one or two of them, and the
   honest empty day. Serves laws 9 and 11.
2. The empty day's own rule: written fresh, never a stored sentence.
   Serves laws 4 and 12, and new ban list entry 43.
3. The daily's traction signal, stated so a 24-hour window can carry
   law 5: a claim leads because today's work connects to something
   alexandria already held, not because it arrived today.

Everything else daily.md is missing, the greeting and the grade and the
close and the link rule, is already house law in digest.md and needs no
new words. It needs the two files to be one file.

## Structure watch

prompt changes alone, the pipeline change goes in the ledger for the
engineer instead. This one cannot be fixed by prompt changes at all. No
edit to digest.md removes a second generator, and porting nine runs of

change: the press loads one craft layer and one cadence file, so a rule
written once binds both issues. I have not edited prompts/daily.md or
anything in PR #35. That file is the engineer's to move and the owner's
to merge, and my saying so in a review is the whole of my remit here.
