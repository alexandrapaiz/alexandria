# Preference data: site copy, 2026-09-29 session

The human companion to `2026-09-29-site-copy.jsonl`. It carries no verdict the
jsonl does not carry.

This is the session that landed. Her verdict on it, recorded in
`docs/voice/taste.md`, is "good. i like this.", and the copy approved in it is
on the site today across home, library, skills, graph and mission. It is the
only approving session the company has ever had on site copy, which makes it
the most valuable preference data the org holds.

**It holds three rows.** That is the finding this page exists to state.

## Why three rows and not twenty

The session was recorded as rules rather than as data. Nobody appended a line
as each verdict arrived, so what survives of it is six rulings in
`docs/voice/taste.md` and the approved words on the site. From that, exactly
three candidates can be quoted the way the schema requires, which is complete
and verbatim and as she ruled on it.

Her ruling (5) names three approved headings in her own words as bare nouns,
and two of them are in the schema's slot list, so those two convert. Her ruling
(3) records one candidate as still rejected and quotes its text, so that
converts as a second ruling on a candidate the 2026-09-20 file already holds.

Everything else in the session was long-form copy, and for long-form copy the
shipped page is not a witness to what she approved. One line proves it. The
home statement she approved on 2026-09-20 reads "What changed in AI research
today, written for people who build things." The same line on the site today
reads "What changed in AI research **this week**, written for people who build
things." The cadence changed under it after she ruled. A page that has drifted
once on the one line where both ends are recorded cannot be trusted as the
candidate text for the lines where only one end is.

So the long-form approvals are not recorded, and their absence is recorded
instead. Writing the shipped paragraph into a `text` field and labelling it
approved would put a sentence she never saw into the one dataset whose whole
purpose is her actual words.

## What that costs, in the dataset's own terms

Schema rule 4 says pairs are derived per slot: every `approved` row pairs with
every `rejected` row in the same slot. Across both session files there are 32
rows and **six derivable pairs**.

| slot | approved | rejected | pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `home.statement` | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| `skills.heading` | 1 | 4 | 4 |
| `library.intro` | 0 | 6 | **0** |
| `skills.intro` | 0 | 2 | **0** |
| `home.para` | 0 | 2 | **0** |
| `home.line2` | 0 | 1 | **0** |
| `library.heading` | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| `skills.line` | 1 | 0 | 0 |

The three slots she rejected most are the three with no approved row. Ten of
the sixteen recorded rejections sit in slots where nothing pairs with them, so a
consumer learns what she refuses there and nothing about what she accepts. The
approvals that would close those three slots were given, on 2026-09-29, and
were not written down as candidates.

## One thing a later reader should not mistake for taste

The approved skills page intro, at `site/app/skills/page.jsx:31`, carries three
sentences that `docs/agents/quality-claims.md` lists in state **not built**:
that the library "keeps every skill current at the pace of the research", and
that a builder gets "agents that work from the current state of the evidence
today and still will next month". Her approval is real and it is not in
question. What is in question is using that paragraph as a model, because the
thing it promises has no mechanism behind it.

This is recorded here rather than acted on. The claim register is the ExO
seat's and the site is the frontend seat's. The writer seat's duty is the one
the charter names: a claim whose row says `not built` ships hedged or does not
ship, and a positive example that fails that test should not be imitated
quietly.

## Slots the schema does not have

The session approved "Claim graph" as the graph page's heading and a graph page
intro, both on the site today. `docs/agents/preference-data.md` has no
`graph.heading` or `graph.intro` in its slot list, and that list is amended "in
the same commit that first uses it" by the seat that owns the file, which is
not this one. Those rows are therefore not written. Filed in `docs/ideas.md`.
