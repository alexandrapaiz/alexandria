This week the most active ideas revolve around two themes. First, feedback‑enriched environments are shown to let agents absorb guidance during training and to explore harder state spaces without extra test‑time tricks. Second, a set of training tricks—dense verification rewards, token‑stream alignment, and a focused reinforcement‑learning loop—push a 122 B Mixture‑of‑Experts terminal agent past the 60% success barrier on long‑horizon benchmarks. Both currents promise more reliable, self‑sufficient agents that need less hand‑crafted inference scaffolding.
 
Trailblazing
Feedback‑Enriched Environments let agents learn guidance and explore without test‑time cues.
The paper introduces environments that add extra observation signals while training. Agents trained this way keep their performance even when those signals are removed at test time, showing that the guidance has been baked into the policy. They also visit broader parts of the state space and succeed more often on sparse‑reward tasks.
· Agents internalize environmental guidance into policy weights rather than relying on inference‑time priors.
· Exploration covers larger state regions, raising success rates on difficult tasks.
· Training becomes more stable because intra‑group feedback consistency reduces entropy volatility.
Environments as Scaffold: Enriching Feedback to Bootstrap Self‑Evolving Agents in Long‑Horizon Tasks
arxiv.org/abs/2609.08404
Training with a dense verification reward lifts task resolution by 28% relative to the supervised baseline.
A dense verification reward counts how many logical assertions a generated answer satisfies. The reward is scaled globally and fed to the critic at the final token, then the actor is updated with PPO using a learning‑rate ratio of 30 : 1. This simple change lets the agent improve from 49.4% to 64.0% resolved tasks after three PPO epochs, while a binary reward never beats the baseline.
1. For each task, a held‑out verifier emits per‑assertion outcomes.
2. The dense reward equals the number of passed assertions.
3. The reward is scaled on a fixed global scale shared across all tasks.
4. The scaled reward is given to the critic at the final response token.
5. The critic learns with a learning rate 30 × the actor’s, then the actor is updated via PPO using the dense reward.
T1: Terminal Agent Reinforcement Learning for Long‑Horizon Tasks
arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042
Aligning training and inference token streams cuts the probability gap and eliminates token drift.
The authors propose two mechanisms. Token‑In‑Token‑Out (TITO) preserves token prefixes across turn boundaries and masks loss to keep continuity. Rollout‑Routing‑Replay (R³) records expert routing decisions during rollouts and replays them during training updates. Together they shrink the log‑probability difference between training and inference from 0.021 to 0.013 and achieve zero token drift in the loss region.
1. Preserve token prefixes across turn boundaries (Token‑In) to avoid re‑encoding drift.
2. Stitch token streams at turn boundaries with loss masking (Token‑Out) to keep continuity.
3. Record per‑token expert routing decisions during each rollout.
4. Replay the recorded routing decisions during training updates.
5. Apply loss masking so only sampled tokens contribute to the loss.
T1: Terminal Agent Reinforcement Learning for Long‑Horizon Tasks
arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042
A 122 B Mixture‑of‑Experts terminal agent resolves 64% of tasks on the standard benchmark.
Starting from a supervised checkpoint at 49.4% resolved, the authors run three epochs of PPO with the dense verification reward described above. After training, the agent outperforms GPT‑3.5‑Turbo (54.8%) and DeepSeek‑V4‑Flash (56.9%) and comes close to Claude Opus 4.7 (66.1%). The result shows that a focused RL loop can bridge most of the gap to the strongest proprietary models.
1. Begin from a supervised checkpoint that resolves 49.4% of benchmark tasks.
2. Run three PPO epochs on a quality‑filtered dataset of 15 k examples.
3. Use the dense verification reward to guide the critic and actor.
4. Update the actor after each critic step.
5. Evaluate on the benchmark; the final resolution rate is 64.0%.
