# Market report — September 2026

Written by the market research agent on its first run, 2026-09-18.
Method: public web surfaces only, every input linked, plus a hands-on
walkthrough of alexandria's own member site built from source at commit
169d806. Findings that flatter alexandria were held to stronger sourcing
than findings that sting, per charter.

## 1. Market definition

alexandria sells recurring AI research intelligence to individual
practitioners in two forms, a $10/mo judgment digest and a $30/mo
library of agent-loadable skills backed by a claim graph. That places it
at the intersection of three markets that today do not overlap:

1. **AI research intelligence tools.** Search and synthesis over the
   scientific literature (Elicit, Consensus, Semantic Scholar, Exa).
   Individual pricing runs free to $49/mo.
2. **AI digests and technical newsletters.** Coverage and editorial
   judgment delivered by email (TLDR AI, AINews, Import AI, The Batch,
   AlphaSignal, Latent Space), almost entirely free and ad-funded, with
   The Pragmatic Engineer proving the paid ceiling at $15/mo.
3. **Agent skills and knowledge ecosystems.** Packaged procedural
   knowledge agents load (the Agent Skills open standard, skills.sh,
   SkillsMP, Smithery). One year old, enormous in volume, and entirely
   free so far.

No observed product spans all three. The digest competes in market 2,
the library tier is an early entrant in a paid layer of market 3 that
does not exist yet, and market 1 supplies the credibility standard the
product is judged against.

## 2. Market size

These are reasoned estimates with arithmetic shown. Confidence is
moderate on orders of magnitude and low on point values.

### TAM, the arena ceiling

- Global developers: 47.2M ([SlashData, 2026](https://www.slashdata.co/post/rapid-growth-in-edge-ai-developers-and-where-the-opportunity-lies)).
- Developers building or integrating AI: roughly 11M by SlashData's
  edge-AI accounting (same source), consistent with 7.3M AI developers
  counted as cloud native ([CNCF/SlashData, March 2026](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/03/24/cncf-and-slashdata-report-finds-cloud-native-community-reaches-nearly-20-million-developers/)).
  Call the addressable population ~12M once technical leaders who do
  not code daily are included.
- At alexandria's blended ~$150/yr: 12M x $150 ≈ **$1.8B/yr**. This is
  the standard top-down ceiling, useful only to say the arena is large.

### SAM, what is actually bought today

Two triangulations.

- Bottom-up from audience: the engaged free AI-newsletter audience is
  roughly 1.5M unique readers after heavy dedupe (TLDR AI claims 1.1M
  ([tldr.tech/ai](https://tldr.tech/ai)), AINews 150k
  ([news.smol.ai](https://news.smol.ai/)), Latent Space 200k+
  ([latent.space/about](https://www.latent.space/about)), The Batch and
  AlphaSignal undisclosed). Substack's platform-wide paid share of
  active subscriptions runs near 5.7%
  ([Backlinko's Substack statistics](https://backlinko.com/substack-users)).
  Applying a 4% conversion to 1.5M gives ~60k people paying for
  AI-specific content, and at a $140/yr blend (between Stratechery's
  $120 and Pragmatic Engineer's $150) that is **~$8.4M/yr** in paid
  AI-practitioner newsletter spend.
- Cross-check from platforms: Substack passed 8.4M paid subscriptions
  in Q1 2026 (same Backlinko source) and beehiiv's paid-subscription
  revenue grew 138% to $19M in 2025 with ~$35M projected for 2026
  ([beehiiv State of Paid Newsletters 2026](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026)).
  Technology is one of the top paid categories. These figures support a
  paid technical-newsletter market in the low hundreds of millions, of
  which the AI-practitioner slice consistent with the bottom-up number
  is a single-digit-percent sliver.
- Add the prosumer research-tool layer alexandria's $30 tier borrows
  willingness-to-pay from: Consensus claims 5M+ registered users
  ([consensus.app/pricing](https://consensus.app/pricing)) at ~$10/mo
  Pro pricing (figures via
  [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-research-tools/consensus/)),
  and a 1-2% paid rate would alone be $6-12M/yr.

**SAM estimate: $20-40M/yr today** for individual-paid AI research
intelligence (digest plus prosumer tools), with growth signals well
above 50%/yr on the paid-newsletter side.

### SOM, the three-year obtainable

A new entrant with no distribution converts credibility into a free
list first. A realistic free list for a differentiated product with a
public-repo credibility engine is 5,000-20,000 readers within two to
three years (for scale, Pragmatic Engineer took years to reach 1M+ and
sits at the category top). At the observed 2-5% paid conversion and a
$12/mo blend weighted to the $10 tier:

- Low: 5,000 x 2% x $144/yr ≈ **$14k ARR**
- Mid: 10,000 x 4% x $144/yr ≈ **$58k ARR**
- High: 20,000 x 5% x $144/yr ≈ **$144k ARR**

On a ~$0 cost base nearly all of that is margin, which is the model's
real strength: the business clears its bar at subscriber counts that
would kill a funded competitor.

## 3. Customer segments

Supported by the audience evidence above and the demand signals in §5.

1. **AI engineers and agent builders** (primary). They already read
   TLDR/AINews free, already install skills (skills.sh top listings
   show 0.9M-3.4M installs, [skills.sh](https://skills.sh/)), and are
   the only segment the $30 tier makes sense for. Buying trigger:
   an agent underperforms and they need current, trusted practice.
2. **Technical leaders and staff+ engineers** (digest tier). They pay
   for judgment today (Pragmatic Engineer's 1M+ readers,
   [about page](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/about)).
   Buying trigger: needing a defensible answer to "what should we be
   doing about X" without reading papers.
3. **Applied researchers and research-adjacent PMs** (digest tier,
   smaller). Currently served by Elicit/Consensus for search but by
   nobody for a maintained record of what held up.
4. **Agents themselves as a delivery surface, not a wallet.** The
   skills standard's client showcase spans dozens of products
   ([agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/)). The human pays, the
   agent consumes. No observed competitor treats this as a paid
   channel yet.

## 5. Trends and unmet needs

**Trends that matter.**

1. **Skills became an open standard and instantly commoditized.**
   Anthropic released the format as an open standard, dozens of agent
   products adopted it ([agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/)), and
   directories now count listings in the hundreds of thousands with
   minimal curation ([skillsmp.com](https://skillsmp.com/),
   [directory survey](https://localskills.sh/blog/claude-skills-marketplace-guide)).
   Supply is infinite. Trust is the scarce good, and nobody sells it.
2. **Free coverage won the news layer.** Six-plus quality free digests
   compete on speed and volume. The only paid wins in adjacent tech
   media sell depth and judgment (Pragmatic Engineer, Stratechery,
   Lenny's at $15-20/mo).
3. **Paid newsletters are compounding.** Substack paid subs grew from
   5M to 8.4M in about a year
   ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/substack-users)) and beehiiv's
   paid revenue more than doubled
   ([beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026)).
   The habit of paying individuals for niche expertise is normalizing.
4. **Research tools are drifting enterprise.** Elicit's ladder tops at
   $169/mo plus enterprise, leaving the individual practitioner at the
   $10-30 band underserved by the tools class.
5. **Content is starting to publish for agents.** agentskills.io ships
   an llms.txt index for machine readers. Machine-consumable knowledge
   products are becoming a distribution surface, which is exactly the
   $30 tier's shape.

**Clearest unmet needs.**

1. **Retrospective verdicts.** Practitioners say the volume is
   unmanageable, "keeping up with everything in AI is impossible" as
   one HN commenter put it
   ([HN, July 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48939630),
   with the same complaint recurring since at least 2024,
   [HN 2024](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41226035)). Every
   product answers "what is new." None answers "what should I now
   believe, and what did we stop believing." That second question is
   alexandria's digest.
2. **Trusted skills.** 800k+ scraped skills and zero evidence trails.
   A skill with claim-level provenance, a validation A/B note, and a
   deprecation policy has no competitor on any directory observed.
3. **Noise-free by construction.** Readers explicitly distrust the
   firehose channels ("X/Twitter is too irony/vaguepost filled... makes
   you less informed," same HN thread). A ledger drawn from the
   literature rather than the feed is a real differentiation, if the
   quality bar holds.

## 6. Product walkthrough — alexandria as a skeptical prospect

Method: built the member site from `site/` at commit 169d806 (clean
`next build`, all routes render) and walked home, library, skills,
graph, pricing, mission, routines, and desk as a stranger would. Also
read `skills/harness-engineering/SKILL.md` and the public repo surface.
Honest limits: the production deployment was not observable (no deploy
URL in the repo, and the ledger lists deploy as accepted but unbuilt),
digests are email-only and gitignored so no issue content was visible
to me, and the database is private. Nothing below guesses at those.

**Where it beats the profiled class.**

- The mission and graph pages read better than most competitor
  marketing. "A graph that never forgets" and the mission's "research
  on the research" framing state a differentiated claim no profiled
  digest can make.
- The harness-engineering skill is materially better than the skills
  ecosystem's median artifact: frontmatter carries claim IDs, five
  linked papers, a dated validation A/B result, and explicit caveats.
  Nothing observed on skills.sh or SkillsMP carries anything similar.
- The billing-honesty stance on the pricing page (renewal reminders
  over silent autorenew) is a genuine trust differentiator no comp
  advertises.
- The graph teaser uses a real structure-only snapshot (254 claims, 88
  edges), which is the right way to tease without leaking product.

**Where it falls short of the class.**

- Both homepage CTAs dead-end. Subscribe leads to pricing marked
  "Coming soon" with no email capture anywhere, and Read an issue leads
  to an archive that renders empty because issue bodies never reach the
  public build. A skeptical prospect can leave nothing and read
  nothing. Every profiled paid comp converts through free samples
  (Pragmatic Engineer's partial deepdives are the reference mechanic).
- The library tier promises "the full skill library" with exactly one
  skill live. At n=1 the $30 pitch is a promise, not a product.
- The hero metric "3,431 papers ingested this week" is hardcoded in
  `site/app/page.jsx`. The repo is public and the audience reads
  source. A static number styled as live telemetry is the kind of
  detail that costs this audience's trust.
- No author, no track record, no social proof anywhere on the site,
  against comps fronted by Karpathy endorsements and named experts.
- Email-only digests mean no shareable issue URLs, no SEO surface, and
  no forwarding loop, while every growing comp compounds through
  public archives.

**What a stranger needs to see before paying.** One complete free
issue, at least three to five skills with validation trails, a live
metric that is actually live, a way to hand over an email today, and
one external proof point (a benchmark, an endorsement, or a public
before/after like the skill's A/B note, surfaced on the site rather
than buried in frontmatter).

## 7. Competitive position and what must be true

alexandria's position is honest and narrow: it cannot outcover TLDR,
outrank skills.sh on volume, or outsearch Elicit, and it should not
try. Its two structural assets are a never-rewritten claim graph and a
validation trail on every skill, and both compound with time, which is
the moat volume players cannot copy backward. The ~$0 cost base means
it wins at subscriber counts irrelevant to every funded comp.

For the $10 tier to win: the digest must visibly beat the free class
on judgment (verdicts with evidence, not summaries), at least one full
issue must be publicly readable at all times, and the monthly OKR
benchmark against the TLDR/Import AI class must show it. Willingness
to pay $10 is already market-proven at this exact price point.

For the $30 tier to win: the skill library must reach a credible
minimum (double digits), each skill must ship with its validation
evidence displayed, and at least one public demonstration must show a
measurable gain from loading an alexandria skill versus a free
marketplace skill on the same task. The tier is a category of one in
an empty $21-44 price band, so its risk is education, not price.

Confidence in the market size: moderate at order-of-magnitude level.
Confidence in the competitive read: high, all surfaces visited
directly. Confidence in the product critique: high on what was
observable, with the stated limits.
