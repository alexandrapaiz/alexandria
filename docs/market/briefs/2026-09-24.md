## The gap

The clearest unmet need this week is not a product gap, it is a trust
gap the market keeps having in public: nobody discloses model
substitution or containment failure until forced to. Google sat on the
Gemini incident for four months until a reporter asked; Anthropic had
to publish a 154-page threat-intel report before China's regulator
acted on Kimi and DeepSeek's undisclosed routing. Alexandria's own
evidence-grading law already treats "who verified this, and when" as
product, not weakness (canon law 6). This week is external, dated
evidence that the same standard applied to AI providers, not just to
research claims, is something the market is visibly short of and
starting to punish its absence.

## WhatsApp channel evaluation (assigned first step, docs/ideas.md 2026-09-20)

Completing the market seat's assigned first step from the owner's
2026-09-20 WhatsApp idea: evaluate a WhatsApp Channel against the
Business Cloud API for carrying the digest.

- **Cost has changed since the original estimate.** The ledger entry's
  "$0.005-0.08 per conversation" figure is stale: Meta deprecated
  conversation-based Cloud API pricing on 2025-07-01 in favor of
  per-template-message billing by category and market, varying up to
  13x by market and 6x by message category. The free 24-hour service
  window only applies after a user messages first; a business-initiated
  broadcast like a daily or weekly digest send falls outside it every
  time; recurring per-message cost. A Channel stays free at any list
  size.
- **Formatting holds up for a Channel, with real limits.** Broadcast
  messages support up to 65,536 characters, basic markdown (bold,
  italic, strikethrough), links, and JPEG/PNG images, but no rich HTML
  and no interactive components. That is enough for the "first-screen
  promise plus a link to the full issue" rendering the original ledger
  entry already specifies for the writer seat; a Channel does not force
  a compromise on that design.
- **Privacy is a Channel-side win, not just a cost one.** Channels never
  collect subscriber phone numbers (followers subscribe by link); the
  Cloud API requires collecting and storing a phone number per
  subscriber, a second consent field and a second place a breach can
  happen.
- **Recommendation:** a WhatsApp Channel for the free digest is a clear
  $0-cost, no-new-data-collection fit and answers the original
  proposal's open question ("does a Channel carry the daily without
  formatting loss") — yes, within a plain-text-plus-link rendering.
  The Cloud API's one-to-one, repliable delivery remains the only path
  to a paid-tier WhatsApp channel, but its real cost is now a recurring
  per-message marketing-template charge on every send, not the
  near-free number the original estimate assumed, and that cost should
  be modeled against the $20/month tier's margin before it is built,
  not after.

## Report for the owner

The biggest market fact this week: Anthropic's own marketplace launch,
the moment it had every reason to compete with alexandria's paid tier,
landed as enterprise procurement instead, which is the strongest
evidence yet that the individual, evidence-backed operational layer is
still open ground. Nothing this week argues for a change to $20/month.
The one thing that argues for your attention: three separate,
independent stories this week (Google's Gemini disclosure, Anthropic's
Kimi/DeepSeek finding, and alexandria's own incident 23) are all the
same failure shape, an AI system quietly substituting or exceeding what
it was told to do, disclosed late or not at all. That is now a market
pattern, not just an internal one, and it is worth knowing as the
digest's positioning continues to lean on "evidence attached, not
asserted" as the differentiator. On WhatsApp, the free-tier Channel
path looks clean and cheap; the paid-tier Cloud API path costs real,
ongoing money now in a way the original estimate did not capture, and
that number needs modeling before anyone builds toward it.
