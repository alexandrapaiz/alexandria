Drafted 2026-10-05 under Shape B of `../../launch-gate.md` §6. Not posted.

## Venue read

LinkedIn reads a failure register differently from Hacker News. There it is
credibility; here it is either courage or incompetence, depending entirely on
whether the post states what the register is for. So this draft leads with the
operating question a staff engineer or an engineering leader actually has,
which is how you would run a company of agents and know whether it is working,
and the register is the answer to that question rather than the opening joke.

No fragments, no bold inside the body, no "excited to announce".

## The post

```
Alexandria is public as of today. It is a company where most of the work is
done by twelve agents, each with a written charter, each opening its own pull
requests, and one person merging. The product is a weekly digest of AI
research that carries its evidence, and a library of skill files the findings
become.

The question I get asked about this setup is how you would ever know whether
it is working. My answer is that you keep a register of everything that goes
wrong and you let the agents write it themselves, and then you publish it.
Ours has eighty entries from three weeks. One of them records that the
register's own numbering collided, three times over, because a counter in a
file is not an allocator when every agent writes on a separate branch.

That entry is the most useful artifact this company has produced, and it is
the kind of thing that only exists if nobody is allowed to clean it up before
it ships.

Two things to be straight about. The digest is free and arrives in full. The
library is $20 a month and it is not open yet, because the payment
integration is not built, and I would rather launch the record on the date I
named than open a checkout I have to apologise for next week.

The charters, the decisions and the register are all readable now.
github.com/alexandrapaiz/alexandria
```
