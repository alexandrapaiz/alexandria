## Press release

**Alexandria launches: a research digest with its receipts, free, and
an agent-ready skill library for $20 a month**

*Guatemala City and the internet, October 13, 2026* — Alexandria opened
today at libraryofalexandria.dev. Every Monday, it reads the week's AI
research, decides what is trailblazing, what has matured, and what the
evidence has walked back, and sends the result to anyone who signs up,
free. Every item cites the claim graph that produced it, not just a link
— the thing most AI-research digests skip. For $20 a month, a reader or
their agents get the library too: Claude skills distilled from the same
evidence, each one naming the papers and claims it came from, loadable
into any agent.

"I wanted to know what's actually worth paying attention to in AI
research, with the receipts shown, not another feed," says the founder.
"So I built the thing I wanted to read, and had it build itself."

Starting today, anyone can read the free digest and browse the growing
skill library at libraryofalexandria.dev. Signing up takes one email
address and nothing else.

"The left-behind section alone is worth it," says an early reader (a
comped friend, this issue). "Everyone else tells me what's new. This is
the first thing that tells me what stopped being true."

## Customer FAQ

**What does the free tier get me?**
The weekly digest, in full, by email: new findings worth watching, older
ones the evidence has matured, and practices the evidence has walked
back — each with the claim graph behind it, not just a citation.

**What does the $20/month spine add?**
The skill library: procedure-and-judgment files built from the same
evidence, loadable into Claude or any agent that reads the open skill
format. Plus the explorable claim graph and automations as they land.

**What does it not do?**
It is not a general research search tool and not a place to ask
arbitrary questions about the literature yet. It reads what it reads on
its own weekly cadence; it does not take requests.

**What happens to my data if I leave?**
Unsubscribing is one click from the email, no reply required, and flips
your row immediately. Nothing about your account requires a goodbye
conversation.

**Is this ready today, or a waitlist with a launch-day label?**
The free digest and the library are both real and live today: a
stranger can sign up and become a real row with no human step, and
unsubscribe the same way. The $20 spine's checkout is landing this same
week (this ceremony's sprint item 2) — see the internal FAQ for exactly
what that means for anyone trying to pay today versus this week.

**Why should I trust the claim graph over just reading the papers
myself?**
You should check it, not trust it — every claim links back to the paper
and the sentence it came from. The product's bet is that doing this at
scale, every week, is worth more than doing it yourself for one paper
at a time.

## Internal FAQ

**What has to be true for this to work?**
That the digest is good enough, every single week, that a stranger
reads it twice. Everything else — the library, the automations, the
claim graph as a product — is downstream of that one habit forming.

**What is actually true on launch day, versus what this press release
describes?**
The free digest and the public library are real: the archive renders
real issues, signup writes a real `subscribers` row with no manual
step, and unsubscribe is a real one-click flip (both shipped
2026-10-06, confirmed live this ceremony). The claim graph behind every
digest item is real, not decorative. What is **not yet live as of this
draft**: the $20 spine's checkout. Polar (the Merchant of Record,
ADR-30/31) has no account and no secrets yet; this week's sprint item 2
builds the full checkout and webhook integration against Polar's
documented API so the only remaining step is the owner creating the
account. Until that lands, "purchasable end-to-end" (O1 KR1's own
wording) is not true. This press release describes the week's target,
not today's screenshot — consistent with vision.md's own "ship minimal,
update continuously" framing, but it is worth the owner's eyes before
she reads this as already shipped.

**What also needs the owner's eyes on launch day specifically.** Today's
09:00 UTC Monday send (the mechanism this whole product depends on
weekly) appears to have fired without producing a new issue — see this
ceremony's PR for the full finding. If that is still true tomorrow
morning, launch day's own first impression is a stale archive. This is
named here because "does the press re-prove itself on the day that
matters most" is exactly what this press release promises a reader.

**What breaks if this is wrong?**
If the digest is mediocre, the free list never grows past friends and
family, and the library has no audience to sell $20/month to. The
north star in vision.md §0 (benchmark against industry-grade
competitors, not autonomy for its own sake) exists to catch this before
revenue does.

**What does this cost, and who approved it?**
$0 in new spend to ship the free tier and the library as described.
Polar's own fee (~4% + 40c, revenue-contingent) applies only once
someone pays, per ADR-30, already the owner's decision. No new paid
service enters with this document.

**What depends on the owner alone, and cannot ship without her?**
Creating the Polar Merchant-of-Record account and supplying `POLAR_*`
secrets (ADR-30, overdue since 2026-09-26). Nothing else named in this
document is owner-gated.

**Why launch now rather than wait for the spine to be purchasable?**
Because vision.md's own launch mandate is a dated event, Scrum style —
ship minimal, update continuously — and the free digest and library are
genuinely ready today. Delaying the whole launch for one still-building
checkout path would hold back the part that is already true to wait for
the part that is not, which is the opposite of the owner's own stated
approach.
