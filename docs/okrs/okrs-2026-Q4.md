## Scoring convention (Doerr/Google, adopted 2026-09-18)

Every KR below is tagged **[Committed]** or **[Aspirational]**, the
distinction Google and John Doerr's *Measure What Matters* draw between
must-hit delivery and deliberate stretch:

- **Committed** KRs are graded 0.0-1.0 against 100% completion. A
  committed KR scoring under 0.8 is a planning or execution failure,
  not an acceptable miss, and the check-in must say which.
- **Aspirational** KRs are graded the same 0.0-1.0 scale but a score
  around 0.7 is success by design, not a shortfall; scoring a 1.0 on an
  aspirational KR means it was set too low next quarter.
- Objective scores are the average of their KRs' scores. The quarter
  average across all KRs is expected to land near 0.6-0.7; consistently
  scoring 1.0 across the board is itself a signal the bar is too low,
  per the source below.
- Source: Google's re:Work OKR guide
  (rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs) and Doerr,
  *Measure What Matters* (2018), on committed vs. aspirational grading
  and the 0.6-0.7 sweet spot.

## O1 — Launch on the owner's date and win the digest class on judgment

The benchmark objective, now also the launch objective: the owner set
2026-10-13 as the launch date at the first all-hands, Scrum style, ship
minimal and update continuously. The digest already has the one thing
TLDR AI and its class lack, an evidence trail, but it is invisible: one
edition sent, email only, no archive, no signup page, no deployed site.
Q4 makes the judgment advantage undeniable, puts a real surface under
it, and gets the business live and paying for itself on the date the
owner named. The consecutive-send reliability this file's first draft
tracked as its own KR here now lives under O3 KR3 instead, which
already counts every manually-triggered send as a drift finding, so it
is not duplicated across two objectives.

- KR1 [Committed]: By 2026-10-13, the site is public with a digest
  archive and the skill library; free digest signup (full issues) and
  the $20/month paid spine are both purchasable end-to-end the same
  day, no manual steps. Supersedes this file's earlier 2026-10-31 date
  and vision §4's $10/$30 tiers, both overtaken by the all-hands.
- KR2 [Committed]: Profitable at launch. By 2026-10-13, that month's
  hosting, API, and vendor costs are covered by paid-spine revenue and
  committed pledges; if not, the shortfall in dollars and the owner's
  subsidy decision are logged in the October check-in rather than
  left unstated.
- KR3 [Committed]: Every digest item cites its evidence. A monthly
  audit of ten sampled items finds ten of ten carrying at least one
  claim-graph edge or citation-trend datum, all three monthly audits
  (Oct, Nov, Dec) passing. This is a quality floor, not a stretch,
  hence committed rather than aspirational.

Tracked but not graded as a KR (a growth number, not the north star):
the free list reaching at least 50 subscribers by 2026-12-31, watched
in each monthly check-in as a read on whether the launch surface is
actually reaching anyone.

## O2 — Make the skill library a real asset, not a proof of concept

The business objective. The gold layer holds exactly one skill. It is a
good one, provenance block, twelve claims, five papers, a recorded A/B
validation, and that format is the moat, because nothing in Anthropic's
176k-star ecosystem carries an evidence trail. One skill is a specimen.
Twelve skills with that rigor is the substance of the $20/month paid
spine the all-hands decided, not a $30 tier as this file's first draft
said before that decision landed.

- KR1 [Aspirational, target ~0.7]: Twelve skills in gold by 2026-12-31,
  each with a full provenance block naming its claims, papers, and
  validation. Nine to ten is a real result; twelve is the stretch.
- KR2 [Committed]: prompts/skill-extract.md is shipped and the pipeline
  produces at least one draft skill per week from claim clusters from
  2026-11-01 onward.
- KR3 [Aspirational, target ~0.7]: Every skill promoted this quarter
  carries a recorded A/B validation before promotion, none promoted on
  judgment alone. Scored against however many KR1 actually promotes,
  not against twelve.

## O3 — Close the autonomy loop from proposal to merge

every promotion and every self-improvement still waits on a human. The
reviewer panel (ADR-13) and the meta-review loop are designed and
accepted in the ledger, and neither is built. Q4 builds them.

- KR1 [Aspirational, target ~0.7]: The reviewer panel (provenance,
  adversary, validator) is live with structured verdict rows by
  2026-11-15, and the first unanimous autonomous merge of a promotion
  PR lands by 2026-12-15. Blocked today on the owner minting a

- KR2 [Aspirational, target ~0.7]: The weekly agent runs as a scheduled
  task producing skill PRs by 2026-10-15, and the meta-review loop
  merges its first self-improvement PR by 2026-12-31.
- KR3 [Committed]: The monthly drift audit finds zero manual
  substitutions in November and December: every digest send, publish,
  backfill, and promotion ran from a cron or action, not from the

### Drift

The finding that matters most this month is not an orphan objective
or an orphan work item. It is a merge bottleneck, and it got worse
while this run was in progress. Nineteen PRs were open against main
when this run started; 28 are open as I write this, oldest from
2026-09-18 (PR #31, six days old), and no product PR has merged into
main since 2026-09-19 (PR #53, accounts plumbing). Everything that
has merged since then is company and fleet-management material: ADR
renumbering, a chair dispatch, incident records, a lessons-register
sync, a transcript-upload change. 17 of the 28 open PRs append to
docs/ideas.md, which means the owner faces a real merge conflict on
that file at every single merge from here, not the hypothetical one
the charter's Act section warns about. This run's own PR does not
touch docs/ideas.md, so it carries no merge-order dependency on any
of the 17. The rate this is compounding at (nine more PRs opened in
roughly the time it took to write this check-in) means whatever exact
count the owner reads this at, the count above is already stale
low, not stale high.

This is not a sprint failing to serve its objectives. Sprint
2026-09-21's five items map cleanly to O1 and O2, and the
quality-first re-triage the owner ordered on 2026-09-18 (ADR-26) is
visibly being followed in the backlog order. It is capacity: the
fleet is producing at a rate the review-and-merge step cannot absorb,
and every KR above that depends on "shipped to main" is being
throttled by that step rather than by the work itself. Worth naming
against the autonomy tiebreak too: nothing here is a manual
substitution for the system's own capability. The opposite problem is
happening. The system is producing faster than its one merge gate can
clear.

One more item for the record, not this month's to grade. ADR-29
(2026-09-19, after this file's Q4 OKRs were drafted) requires "the
OKR seat's October run must carry a coverage key result" derived from
the zero-gap mandate's four hunter classes. The charter's own rule is
that objectives do not change outside quarter turns, and Q4's
quarter-turn ceremony already ran in September, before ADR-29
existed. October's run needs to reconcile these two rules, most
likely by sharpening an existing KR's wording (O3 KR3's drift audit
is the natural home for the four gap classes) rather than adding a
fourth KR outright, but that is October's call to make, not this
run's to preempt.
