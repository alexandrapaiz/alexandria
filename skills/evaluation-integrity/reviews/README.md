# evaluation-integrity: consumer reports

The lane is open and empty. File as `YYYY-MM-DD-<consumer>.md` against the
contract in
[skills/_validation/reviews/README.md](../../_validation/reviews/README.md).

Most wanted. This file was the longest in the library until 2026-11-01, when the
ADR-38 retrofit took it from 477 lines to 119 and from ten sections to five
deltas. No delta has been exercised by anything but a claim id, and none has a
differential number, because this seat still has no route to either eval arm.
The three that would move most on one report:

- **Delta 1, the rubric is an attack surface.** The four-step attack is cheap to
  run. Anyone who ran it on their own rubrics should report the exploit rate and,
  more importantly, which oracle produced it, because swapping the oracle moved
  the published rate across 33.3, 75.6 and 66.7 percent.
- **Delta 4, the panel.** Three of the four portable parts are marked as ours
  rather than the paper's. A report saying which of the three survived contact is
  the evidence they do not have.
- **Delta 5, the noise band.** The five-repeat floor is ours and it is a guess. A
  report naming the number of repeats it actually took to see a spread would
  replace a guess with a measurement.

Two things a report here is worth more than usual for. Say whether the retrofit
cut too far: the five deltas were compressed from ten sections, and a reader who
needed something that is no longer here is the only evidence that the cut was
wrong. The last caveat names exactly what left and where it went. And say whether
a delta was confirmatory rather than decision-changing, because ADR-38 makes
several zero-decision-change reports a deprecation candidate exactly as a
regressed eval would, and this skill has no eval numbers to regress yet.
